# Coordinate-initial regular first-order six-block zero theorem

## Status and claim boundary

`PROOF_DRAFT_COMPLETE`, `CHARACTERISTIC_ZERO`,
`EXACT_54,264_FRAME_REPLAY`, `RESTRICTED_ROUTE_THEOREM`.

This note closes the entire **coordinate-initial regular first-order**
six-block stratum at the open quartic frontier. It does not close second- or
higher-order coordinate degenerations, noncoordinate initial frames, singular
or Puiseux families, or the unrestricted value of `mu(6,4)`.

Let six degree-six Chow terms specialize at order zero to products of matrix
coordinate variables, with repetitions allowed. Let

\[
h_i(t)\in\mathcal D_4(T_i(t))
\]

be regular one-parameter families satisfying

\[
\sum_{i=1}^6 h_i(0)=0.
\tag{0.1}
\]

Then the matching projection of

\[
\sum_{i=1}^6 h_i'(0)
\]

cannot have all 24 perfect-matching coordinates nonzero. In particular it
cannot equal a nonzero diagonal-torus transform of `perm_4`.

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\text{no coordinate-initial regular first-order six-block lift produces }
\operatorname{perm}_4.
}
\tag{0.2}
\]

## 1. One coordinate frame

Fix one component. Its six factor labels form a vector space `F`, and the
order-zero coordinate specialization defines

\[
\mu:\operatorname{Sq}^4(F)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}^4(V).
\tag{1.1}
\]

The source has dimension `binom(6,4)=15`. Let `A` be the set of distinct
coordinate cells used by the six factors, so `|A|<=6`. Define

\[
\mathcal F(A)=\{M:|M\cap A|\ge3\},
\tag{1.2}
\]

where `M` ranges over the 24 perfect matchings of the `4 x 4` block.

Every matching monomial appearing after one regular factor motion retains
three unchanged coordinate factors. Motion of the source vector itself can
produce a matching only when all four matching cells occur in the frame.
Therefore every first-order matching coordinate of this component belongs to
`F(A)`.

The exact single-frame envelope theorem gives

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal F(A)|\le6.}
\tag{1.3}
\]

Exhaustion of all 14,893 distinct supports of size at most six gives 288
equality supports. They have six distinct cells, contain no perfect matching,
have row and column degree sequence `(2,2,1,1)`, and form two row-column
orbits of size 144.

The present theorem strengthens this local analysis by retaining the kernel of
(1.1), which is essential when coordinate factors repeat.

## 2. Private first-order matching coordinates

Put

\[
K=\ker\mu.
\]

A matching coordinate is called **private for the frame** if it can be created
without a nonzero order-zero polynomial that must cancel against another
component. There are two mechanisms:

1. source-vector motion `mu(dot w)`, which gives a matching already contained
   in the frame; or
2. factor motion `dot mu(k)` with `k in K`, an internal factor-label
   cancellation invisible in the order-zero polynomial.

Let `P(A)` be the union of these private matching coordinates.

### Lemma 2.1 -- exact private cap

For every multiset of six coordinate factors,

\[
\boxed{|P(A)|\le2.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

The fibers of (1.1) consist of four-label subsets that give the same coordinate
monomial. The zero-sum space in every fiber is generated by elementary
exchanges of two factor labels carrying the same coordinate cell. It is enough
to study one such exchange.

After exchanging equal labels, a first-order perfect matching retains three
other labels. Those labels must carry three distinct, row- and column-disjoint
cells, which uniquely determine the fourth cell of the perfect matching.

Write `s=|A|`.

- If `s=6`, there is no repeated factor, so `K=0`. A six-cell support contains
  at most two perfect matchings.
- If `s=5`, exactly one cell is repeated. Removing the exchanged pair leaves
  four labels. A four-edge bipartite graph has at most two distinct
  completions of three-edge matchings, unless the four edges form a perfect
  matching, in which case all four triples complete to the same matching. If
  the original five-cell support already contains a perfect matching, the
  direct matching and the internal completions still form a set of size at
  most two.
- If `s=4`, the multiplicity pattern is `3+1+1+1`, `2+2+1+1`, or more
  concentrated. Each duplicated cell supplies at most one new three-edge
  completion, and at most two duplicated cells can contribute. If the support
  is itself a perfect matching, every internal completion is the same
  matching.
- If `s<=3`, an elementary exchange leaves too few suitable distinct cells,
  except for at most one completion in the `3+2+1` or `4+1+1` boundary.

Thus direct source motion and internal-kernel factor motion together reach at
most two perfect matchings. QED.

The exact replay independently reconstructs the elementary exchanges in all

\[
\binom{16+6-1}{6}=54,264
\]

unordered multisets of six coordinate cells and confirms (2.1).

### Lemma 2.2 -- envelope-six frames have no private coordinates

If

\[
|\mathcal F(A)|=6,
\]

then the factors occupy six distinct cells, the source map (1.1) is injective,
and the support contains no perfect matching. Hence

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal F(A)|=6\Longrightarrow P(A)=0.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

This is verified on all 288 equality frames and follows from the equality
classification after (1.3).

A complementary check is that any frame containing two perfect matchings has
first-order envelope exactly those two matchings. The two matchings share
exactly two cells. Their six-cell union consists of two common edges and one
alternating four-cycle. Any perfect matching using three union edges is one of
the original pair.

## 3. Why a nonprivate matching needs two envelopes

Return to the six components and write

\[
q_i=\mu_i(w_i)=h_i(0).
\]

Equation (0.1) gives

\[
\sum_i q_i=0
\tag{3.1}
\]

as an equality of quartic polynomials.

Fix a target matching `M` that is not private for any component. Source-vector
motion contributes zero at `M`, and factor motion of every kernel vector also
contributes zero there. Consequently the coefficient of `M` in factor motion
descends from

\[
\operatorname{Sq}^4(F_i)/K_i\cong\operatorname{im}\mu_i.
\]

If component `i` contributes nontrivially at `M`, some order-zero quartic
monomial `Q` has nonzero coefficient in `q_i` and contains three cells of `M`
that remain unchanged under the motion. By (3.1), the same polynomial
monomial `Q` has nonzero coefficient in at least one other `q_j`. Its three
matching cells therefore also belong to frame `j`, so

\[
M\in\mathcal F(A_i)\cap\mathcal F(A_j).
\]

Thus every nonprivate target matching belongs to at least two component
envelopes.

This quotient step is essential. Merely saying that an order-zero source
monomial must be shared would be invalid before internal factor-label kernel
motion is removed.

## 4. Global incidence squeeze

Let

\[
\mathcal F_i=\mathcal F(A_i),
\qquad
P=\bigcup_{i=1}^6P(A_i),
\qquad
p=|P|.
\]

By (1.3),

\[
I:=\sum_{i=1}^6|\mathcal F_i|\le6\cdot6=36.
\tag{4.1}
\]

By Lemma 2.1,

\[
p\le\sum_i|P(A_i)|\le6\cdot2=12.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Suppose the first derivative had all 24 matching coordinates nonzero. A
private matching needs at least one envelope incidence, while every other
matching needs at least two by Section 3. Hence

\[
I\ge p+2(24-p)=48-p\ge36.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Equations (4.1)--(4.3) force equality everywhere:

\[
p=12,
\qquad
I=36.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Every component would therefore have to satisfy simultaneously

\[
|P(A_i)|=2,
\qquad
|\mathcal F(A_i)|=6.
\tag{4.5}
\]

But Lemma 2.2 says every envelope-six frame has private capacity zero. This
contradiction proves (0.2).
