## 2. Scalar first-prolongation growth

For a quadratic space

\[
P_0\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2V
\]

define

\[
P_0^{(1)}
=
\left\{
f\in\operatorname{Sym}^3V:
\partial_\xi f\in P_0
\text{ for every }\xi\in V^*
\right\}.
\]

### Lemma 2.1 — Macaulay cap

If

\[
\dim P_0\le r,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
\dim P_0^{(1)}
\le r^{\langle2\rangle},
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

where `r^{<2>}` is the degree-two Macaulay successor. For the values needed
here,

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
r&0&1&2&3&4&5\\
\hline
r^{\langle2\rangle}&0&1&2&4&5&7.
\end{array}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
W=P_0^{(1)}
\]

and close `W` under all differentiations. This gives a finite graded inverse
system `M`. By apolar inverse-system duality, `M` is the graded dual of a
standard graded Artinian quotient. Its degree-two piece is the derivative
span of `W`, hence has dimension at most `r`, while its degree-three piece
is `W`.

Macaulay growth for the Hilbert function therefore gives

\[
\dim W
\le
(\dim\partial W)^{\langle2\rangle}
\le
r^{\langle2\rangle}.
\]

The displayed values follow from the binomial expansions

\[
3=\binom32,
\qquad
4=\binom32+\binom11,
\qquad
5=\binom32+\binom21.
\]

For example,

\[
5^{\langle2\rangle}
=
\binom43+\binom32
=7.
\]

∎

## 3. Quadratic relations control cubic relations componentwise

Let

\[
\mathcal K
=
\ker\left(
G_1\oplus G_2\oplus G_3\oplus G_4
\longrightarrow U
\right)
\]

be the quadratic relation kernel, and put

\[
\kappa=\dim\mathcal K.
\]

Let

\[
C_i=\mathcal D_3(T_i)
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal R
=
\ker\left(
C_1\oplus C_2\oplus C_3\oplus C_4
\longrightarrow
C_1+\cdots+C_4
\right)
\]

be the cubic relation kernel. Write

\[
\rho=\dim\mathcal R.
\]

For each component let

\[
P_i=\operatorname{pr}_i(\mathcal K)
\subseteq G_i.
\]

### Lemma 3.1 — four-component relation cap

\[
\boxed{
\rho
\le
3\,\kappa^{\langle2\rangle}.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Take a cubic relation

\[
(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)\in\mathcal R.
\]

For every direction `xi`,

\[
(\partial_\xi c_1,\ldots,\partial_\xi c_4)
\in\mathcal K.
\]

Hence

\[
\partial_\xi c_i\in P_i
\qquad
\text{for every }i,\xi,
\]

so

\[
c_i\in P_i^{(1)}.
\]

Set

\[
W_i=P_i^{(1)},
\qquad
w_i=\dim W_i.
\]

Lemma 2.1 gives

\[
w_i\le\kappa^{\langle2\rangle}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\mathcal R
\subseteq
\ker\left(
W_1\oplus\cdots\oplus W_4
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Sym}^3V
\right).
\]

The sum map has rank at least `max_i w_i`, because its restriction to each
individual `W_i` is the natural inclusion. Therefore

\[
\rho
\le
\sum_iw_i-\max_iw_i
\le
3\,\kappa^{\langle2\rangle}.
\]

∎

This is the key improvement over the earlier one- and two-relation
arguments. It does not try to classify ternary cubic relation components.
It bounds the entire cubic relation space through scalar Hilbert growth.

## 4. A block-Sylvester lower bound for the coupled central rank

Let

\[
A_i=C_{3,3}(T_i),
\qquad
A=C_{3,3}(R)=A_1+\cdots+A_4.
\]

Use divided-power bases, so every middle catalectic `A_i` is symmetric.
Put

\[
c_i=\operatorname{rank}A_i=\dim C_i.
\]

### Lemma 4.1 — coupled rank from the cubic relation dimension

\[
\boxed{
h=\operatorname{rank}A
\ge
\sum_{i=1}^4c_i-2\rho.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(A_1,A_2,A_3,A_4),
\]

let `Sigma` sum the four target copies, and let `Delta` diagonally embed the
common source. Then

\[
A=\Sigma D\Delta.
\]

Frobenius--Sylvester gives

\[
\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma D\Delta)
\ge
\operatorname{rank}(\Sigma D)
+
\operatorname{rank}(D\Delta)
-
\operatorname{rank}D.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The first rank is

\[
\dim(C_1+\cdots+C_4)
=
\sum_ic_i-\rho.
\]

The second rank is the dimension of the sum of the row spaces. Symmetry of
the middle catalectics makes it the same number. Finally,

\[
\operatorname{rank}D=\sum_ic_i.
\]

Substitution into (4.2) proves (4.1). ∎

Combining Lemmas 3.1 and 4.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
h
\ge
\sum_ic_i
-
6\,\kappa^{\langle2\rangle}.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For one pattern define

\tag{5.3}

The right side is always at most `27-b`.

## 10. Claim boundary

This theorem does not prove

\[
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_6)\ge25,
\]

does not improve the border Chow-rank claim, and does not prove the
conjectural exact value 32.

The finite enumeration checks only the integer defect arithmetic. The
logical responsibility for the result is split explicitly:

1. Macaulay growth gives the scalar prolongation caps;
2. differentiation sends cubic relations into the quadratic relation
   kernel;
3. the block-Sylvester inequality converts relation loss into coupled
   central rank;
4. the standard-library script exhausts the remaining defect patterns.
