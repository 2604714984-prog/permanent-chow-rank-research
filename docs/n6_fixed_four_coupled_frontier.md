# A realizability-aware fixed-four frontier for `n=6`

## 1. Purpose

The one-step multidimensional-shadow formula proved

\[
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_6)\ge23
\]

by fixing four terms and using the scalar intersection cap

\[
b\le40.
\]

The exact continuous optimization of that scalar formula also showed that no choice of witness improves the result beyond 23.

The scalar cap ignores that the four fixed summands are actual Chow terms. The universal one-term theorem now gives the missing realizability input at quadratic degree:

\[
\dim\left(
\mathcal D_2(\operatorname{perm}_6)
\cap
\mathcal D_2(T)
\right)
\le3
\tag{1.1}
\]

for every individual degree-six Chow term `T`.

This note combines (1.1) with a projection argument, Bukh's two-dimensional shadow theorem, and the symmetric middle catalectic. The raw cap `40` drops to

\[
\boxed{b\le27}.
\]

The hypothetical 23-term problem is then reduced to exactly 36 integer states.

## 2. Setup under a hypothetical 23-term decomposition

Assume

\[
P=\operatorname{perm}_6
=T_1+\cdots+T_{23}.
\]

Fix four terms and write

\[
R=T_1+T_2+T_3+T_4,
\qquad
Q=P-R.
\]

Thus `Q` is a sum of 19 Chow terms.

At central degree three, set

\[
E_3=\mathcal D_3(P),
\qquad
H_3=\mathcal D_3(R),
\]

\[
b=\dim(E_3\cap H_3),
\qquad
h=\dim H_3,
\qquad
d=h-b.
\tag{2.1}
\]

At degree two, write

\[
E_2=\mathcal D_2(P),
\qquad
G_i=\mathcal D_2(T_i),
\qquad
U_2=G_1+G_2+G_3+G_4.
\]

The coupled quadratic image of `R` satisfies

\[
\mathcal D_2(R)\subseteq U_2.
\]

For one degree-six Chow term,

\[
\dim G_i\le\binom62=15.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The universal single-term proof gives

\[
\dim(E_2\cap G_i)\le3.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Equation (2.3) is not a random coordinate observation. It follows by degenerating the at-most-six-dimensional factor span to a coordinate subspace and classifying the resulting rectangle space.

## 3. A 48-dimensional upper bound for the quadratic shadow

### Lemma 3.1 — projection bound

\[
\boxed{
\dim(E_2\cap U_2)\le48.
}
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
X=E_2\cap U_2
\]

and choose a linear section

\[
\sigma:X\longrightarrow G_1\oplus G_2\oplus G_3\oplus G_4
\]

of the summation map.

Project `sigma(X)` to any three factors, say `G_1 direct_sum G_2 direct_sum G_3`. The image has dimension at most

\[
3\cdot15=45.
\]

If an element of `X` lies in the kernel of that projection, its chosen lift has only a `G_4` component. Its sum is therefore an element of

\[
E_2\cap G_4,
\]

whose dimension is at most three by (2.3). Thus

\[
\dim X\le45+3=48.
\]

The same argument works with any factor omitted. ∎

Let

\[
S=E_3\cap H_3.
\]

All first derivatives of `S` lie both in `E_2` and in the coupled image `D_2(R)`. Therefore

\[
\partial S
\subseteq
E_2\cap\mathcal D_2(R)
\subseteq
E_2\cap U_2.
\]

Lemma 3.1 gives

\[
\boxed{
\dim\partial S\le48.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

## 4. Bukh shadow compression gives `b<=27`

The central permanent derivative space has the multiplicity-free basis

\[
\left\{
P_{I,J}:
I,J\in\binom{[6]}3
\right\}.
\]

A generic row-column torus degeneration sends the `b`-dimensional subspace `S` to a coordinate family of the same dimension. The first-derivative rank cannot increase in the limit. Bukh's two-dimensional Kruskal--Katona theorem therefore gives the following.

Write

\[
b=\binom{x}{3}^2,
\qquad x\ge3.
\]

Then

\[
\dim\partial S
\ge
\binom{x}{2}^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

To obtain an exact integer cap, use the rational separator

\[
x_0=\frac{427}{100}.
\]

Exact arithmetic gives

\[
\binom{x_0}{3}^2
=
\frac{111624739998121}{4000000000000}
<28,
\tag{4.2}
\]

while

\[
\binom{x_0}{2}^2
=
\frac{19496257641}{400000000}
>48.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Both generalized binomial functions are increasing for `x>=3`. If `b>=28`, then the corresponding `x` is strictly larger than `x_0`, and (4.1)–(4.3) give

\[
\dim\partial S>48,
\]

contradicting (3.2). Hence:

### Proposition 4.1

\[
\boxed{
b\le27.
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

This is a realizability-aware improvement over the scalar Bukh cap 40.

## 5. The symmetric central catalectic gives the lower edge `b>=20`

The central catalectic of `P` has rank 400. The central catalectic of `R` has rank `h`, and its image and row space are both `H_3` because the middle catalectic is symmetric.

The horizontal and vertical concatenations with the permanent catalectic both have rank

\[
400+h-b.
\]

The double-quotient rank inequality therefore yields

\[
\operatorname{rank}C_{3,3}(Q)
\ge
400+h-2b.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The residual `Q` is a sum of 19 Chow terms, each of central catalectic rank at most 20. Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}C_{3,3}(Q)\le19\cdot20=380.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Combining (5.1) and (5.2),

\[
h\le2b-20.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Since `H_3` contains its `b`-dimensional intersection with `E_3`, one has `h>=b`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
20\le b\le27,
\qquad
0\le d=h-b\le b-20.
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

The number of integer pairs `(b,d)` in (5.4) is

\[
1+2+\cdots+8=36.
\]

\[

\[

## 7. What the frontier means

This result changes the next research target materially.

The previous scalar frontier at `b=40` required a quotient gain of at least 661. The realizability-aware projection reduces the maximum intersection to 27, and the largest gain requirement to

\[

\]

However, 21 of the 36 states have `b-d>=22`; no bound on the existing `Gamma=36d-p` can close them. They must be ruled out using geometry of four coupled Chow terms, or by introducing a genuinely stronger flattening.

The next minimal program is therefore:
