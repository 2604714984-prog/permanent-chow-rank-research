## 1. The reference flag point

Let

\[
\mathcal R_r
=
\mathbf k\left\langle\binom{[8]}r\right\rangle,
\qquad
\mathcal C_r
=
\mathbf k\left\langle\binom{[8]}r\right\rangle.
\]

Identify the subpermanent basis of `E_4` with

\[
e_{R,C}=e_R\otimes e_C,
\qquad
R,C\in\binom{[8]}4.
\]

For a cell `(i,j)`, first differentiation is

\[
\partial_{ij}e_{R,C}
=
\begin{cases}
e_{R\setminus\{i\},C\setminus\{j\}},&i\in R,\ j\in C,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{1.1}
\]

Use the coordinate flag

\[
U=\{0,1,\ldots,6\},
\qquad z=7,
\]

\[
V=\{0,1,\ldots,5\},
\qquad C_0=\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]

Put

\[
H_4=\left\langle e_R:R\in\binom U4\right\rangle,
\qquad
Q_4=\left\langle e_C:C\in\binom V4\right\rangle,
\qquad
\ell_4=\langle e_{C_0}\rangle.
\]

The reference equality plane is

\[
S_0
=
H_4\otimes Q_4
+
\mathcal R_4\otimes\ell_4.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Since `ell_4 subset Q_4`, its dimension is

\[
35\cdot15+70-35=560.
\]

Writing `H_3`, `Q_3`, and `ell_3` for the respective first shadows gives

\[
\partial S_0
=
H_3\otimes Q_3
+
\mathcal R_3\otimes\ell_3.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Here

\[
\dim H_3=35,
\quad
\dim Q_3=20,
\quad
\dim\ell_3=4,
\quad
\ell_3\subset Q_3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\dim\partial S_0
=35\cdot20+56\cdot4-35\cdot4
=784.
\tag{1.4}
\]

The coordinate equality theorem proves that the `6,720` torus-fixed points of
`X` form the row-column-permutation-and-transpose orbit of this model.

## 2. The determinantal tangent criterion

Let

\[
\mu_0:S_0\otimes V_8^*\longrightarrow E_3
\]

be first differentiation and write

\[
B=\operatorname{im}\mu_0.
\]

The basis description (1.1) makes `B` a coordinate `784`-plane.
A Grassmann-chart tangent vector is a map

\[
\varphi:S_0\longrightarrow E_4/S_0.
\]

For a basis vector `a in S_0` and a cell `x`, define

\[
D_\varphi(a,x)
=
\partial_x\varphi(a)\pmod B.
\tag{2.1}
\]

### Lemma 2.1 -- first-order factorization

The tangent vector `varphi` lies in `T_(S_0) X` if and only if

\[
D_\varphi|_{\ker\mu_0}=0.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Equivalently:

1. if `partial_x a=0`, then `D_varphi(a,x)=0`;
2. if two nonzero derivative columns satisfy
   `partial_x a=partial_y b`, then
   `D_varphi(a,x)=D_varphi(b,y)`.

### Proof

At a rank-exactly-`784` point, the tangent space to the rank-at-most-`784`
determinantal locus consists of perturbations that send the old kernel into
the old image.  Modding out by `B` gives (2.2).  Since every nonzero column of
`mu_0` is one coordinate vector of `B`, its kernel is generated by zero
columns and differences of columns with the same coordinate image. ∎

## 3. Exact solution of the linear tangent equations

The ambient Grassmann chart has

\[
560(4,900-560)=2,430,400
\]

raw graph variables.  For an outside basis vector `b` and an inside basis
vector `a`, the zero-column condition kills the graph coefficient unless every
cell of `b` whose deletion leaves the shadow `B` lies in the rectangle of
`a`.  Exactly `8,960` variables survive this first filter.

The remaining equations in Lemma 2.1 only identify individual variables or
identify them with zero.  Thus they form a finite union-find problem.  The
exact reconstruction gives:

```text
allowed graph variables                 8,960
variables connected to zero             2,240
variables in nonzero components         6,720
nonzero components / tangent dimension     27
```

The component-size distribution is

```text
385: 8 components
350: 4 components
280: 7 components
 35: 8 components.
```

The twenty-seven components have a direct interpretation.

## 4. Mixed second-order obstruction

Split the target as

\[
E_3=B\oplus C.
\]

For a tangent direction `alpha`, write `D_B^alpha` and `D_C^alpha` for the two
components of its first variation.  Lemma 2.1 gives a unique map on the old
image,

\[
\psi_\alpha:B\longrightarrow C,
\qquad
D_C^\alpha=\psi_\alpha\mu_0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Near `S_0`, a rank-`784` image is the graph of a map from `B` to `C`.  Expanding
the graph equation to mixed degree two shows that two tangent directions
`alpha,beta` can occur together only if

\[
Q_{\alpha,\beta}
=
\psi_\alpha D_B^\beta
+
\psi_\beta D_B^\alpha
\tag{4.2}
\]

factors through `mu_0`.  Any second-order correction to the Grassmann graph
changes (4.2) only by a map that already factors through `mu_0`; hence its
class is the intrinsic mixed Kuranishi obstruction.

As in Section 2, factorization is checked by zero columns and equality across
all preimages of one coordinate vector of `B`.  The exact finite calculation
gives:

```text
unordered direction pairs        351
zero mixed obstructions            42
nonzero mixed obstructions        309
```

The coordinate obstruction equations collapse to exactly `256` distinct
quadrics:

```text
203 monomials
 53 binomials.
```

Their coefficient matrix has rank `256` over `F_1000003`.  Since the same
matrix has integer entries, this is a characteristic-zero lower bound; the
explicit list of only `256` equations is the matching upper bound.  Thus the
quadrics are independent in characteristic zero.

## 6. Set-theoretic solution of the quadrics

The zero set of (5.1)--(5.7) is elementary to classify.

If some `r_u` is nonzero, (5.1) forces all other coordinates to vanish.  This
gives seven row-transvection lines.

Assume now that every `r_u` is zero.  Equations (5.2) and (5.4) imply that at
most one source row of the `6 x 2` matrix `(a_(v,e))` is nonzero.  Indeed, two
nonzero source rows cannot share a nonzero entry in one column by (5.2); if
their nonzero entries lie in opposite columns, their `2 x 2` permanent in
(5.4) is nonzero.  The identical argument with (5.3) and (5.5) shows that at
most one source row of `(ell_(u,w))` is nonzero.

Suppose both matrices are nonzero.  If the unique ambient source `v` lies in
`C_0`, equation (5.6) forces the unique low-line source to be the same `v`.
If `v=4`, equation (5.7) forces the low line to use only target `5`; if `v=5`,
it forces the low line to use only target `4`.

Consequently the quadratic zero set is the union of the following maximal

## 7. Every quadratic component integrates

It remains to show that the spaces in Section 6 are not merely unobstructed to
second order.

For a finite ground set `X`, an element `a in X`, external labels `Y`, and
parameters `t_y`, define the elementary-replacement subspace

\[
Z_r(X;a\rightsquigarrow\mathbf t)
\subseteq
\mathbf k\left\langle\binom{X\cup Y}r\right\rangle
\]

by the basis

\[
e_I\quad(a\notin I),
\]

\[
e_I+\sum_{y\in Y}t_y e_{I\setminus\{a\}\cup\{y\}}
\quad(a\in I).
\tag{7.1}
\]

### Lemma 7.1 -- elementary replacement preserves the shadow profile

\[
\partial Z_r(X;a\rightsquigarrow\mathbf t)
=Z_{r-1}(X;a\rightsquigarrow\mathbf t).
\tag{7.2}
\]

In particular, the dimensions are `binom(|X|,r)` and
`binom(|X|,r-1)`.

### Proof

Differentiate the two basis types in (7.1).  Removing a label other than `a`
produces the corresponding transformed `(r-1)`-basis vector; removing `a` or
one of the external labels produces an unchanged lower basis vector.  This
gives the inclusion from left to right.  Conversely, every unchanged or
transformed `(r-1)`-basis vector is obtained by differentiating a suitable
`r`-basis vector. ∎

For each four-plane in (6.1), move one `u in C_0` simultaneously toward the two
external labels in the fifteen-dimensional high column space, and move the
same `u` arbitrarily toward `4,5,6,7` in the low line.  The low line remains
inside the moving high column space and its four-dimensional shadow remains
inside the moving twenty-dimensional high shadow.

For each three-plane in (6.2), move `4` (respectively `5`) toward the two
external labels in the high column space, while moving one basis element of
`C_0` toward the other internal label `5` (respectively `4`).  Again the low
line and its shadow remain inside the moving high spaces.

For each line in (6.3), apply one elementary replacement to the seven-element
high row space.

In all cases the resulting `560`-plane has the form

\[
S(\mathbf t)
=H_4(\mathbf t)\otimes Q_4(\mathbf t)
+
\mathcal R_4\otimes\ell_4(\mathbf t),
\tag{7.3}
\]

with

\[
\ell_4(\mathbf t)\subseteq Q_4(\mathbf t),
\qquad
\partial\ell_4(\mathbf t)\subseteq\partial Q_4(\mathbf t).
\]

Lemma 7.1 gives dimensions `35`, `20`, and `4` for the three relevant shadow
spaces.  Therefore the same tensor-intersection calculation as (1.4) gives

\[
\dim\partial S(\mathbf t)=784
\]

for every parameter value for which the displayed graph chart is defined.
Thus all nineteen linear spaces lie in the actual tangent cone of `X`.

## 8. Main theorem and global dimension

### Theorem 8.1 -- reduced tangent-cone support

At every torus-fixed flag point of `X`, the support of the reduced tangent cone
is exactly the union (6.1)--(6.3).

### Proof

Every actual arc satisfies the mixed second-order Kuranishi equations, so its
leading tangent direction lies in the quadratic zero set classified in
Section 6.  Conversely, Section 7 integrates every point of every listed
linear space into an exact equality family. ∎

### Corollary 8.2 -- dimension of the equality locus

\[
\boxed{\dim\mathfrak X=4.}
\]

### Proof

The variety `X` is projective and stable under the row-column diagonal torus.
Every irreducible component contains a torus-fixed point.  The weight spaces of
`E_4` are one-dimensional, so fixed Grassmann points are coordinate planes;
the coordinate equality theorem puts every such point in the flag orbit.
Theorem 8.1 shows that the local dimension at every fixed point is at most
four.  Hence every global component has dimension at most four.  The explicit
four-parameter families in (6.1) give the reverse inequality. ∎

## 9. Consequence for Chow realizability

The noncoordinate equality problem at the `perm_8` exact-shadow threshold is
no longer an arbitrary deformation problem in

\[
\operatorname{Gr}(560,4,900).
\]

Every irreducible equality component has dimension at most four, and every
formal arc approaching a coordinate flag begins in one of only nineteen
explicit elementary-replacement types.  The next valid test is therefore:

1. restrict the fourteen-term coupled Chow incidence to the four-dimensional
   top branches;
2. test the eight three-dimensional boundary branches and seven row lines only
   if they occur as specializations;
3. compute whether the coupled derivative image or the next shadow acquires a
   strict defect on each branch.

A proof that no fourteen-term Chow incidence dominates one of the four top
branches would improve the current `perm_8` lower bound.  This note does not
supply that final incidence exclusion.

## 11. Strongest objection and unproved items

The strongest objection is that a tangent-cone classification need not equal
a complete formal or global component classification.  Different arcs may
share one leading direction and separate at higher order; embedded or
nonreduced structure may also remain.  The present theorem deliberately stops
at the reduced tangent-cone support and the global dimension bound.
