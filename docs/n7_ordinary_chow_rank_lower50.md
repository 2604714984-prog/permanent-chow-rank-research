# notation not:apolar-derivatives

## statement

Let \(V\) be the 49-dimensional span of the variables \(x_{ij}\), let
\(S=\operatorname{Sym}(V^*)\) act by constant-coefficient differentiation,
and put

\[
P=\operatorname{perm}_7,\qquad E_d=\mathcal D_d(P),
\]

where \(\mathcal D_d(F)\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^dV\) is the space of
degree-\(d\) derivatives of a degree-seven form \(F\).  Thus

\[
\dim E_d=\binom 7d^2.
\]

For a Chow term \(T\), write \(L(T)\subseteq V\) for the span of its actual
linear factors and \(A(T)=S/T^\perp\) for its apolar algebra.  If
\(P=\sum_{i=1}^{49}T_i\), set

\[
L_i=L(T_i),\quad A_i=A(T_i),\quad
H_d=\sum_i\mathcal D_d(T_i),
\]

\[
J=\bigcap_iT_i^\perp,\quad C=S/J,\quad I=P^\perp,
\quad R=I/J\subset C.
\]

Put \(h_d=\dim H_d-\dim E_d\).  With the standard apolar grading one has

\[
\dim R_d=\dim H_d-\binom 7d^2=h_d.                  \tag{N1}
\]

In particular,

\[
\dim R_3=h_3,\qquad \dim R_4=h_4.                  \tag{N2}
\]

## proof

The degree-\(d\) restriction map
\(S_d\to\bigoplus_i(A_i)_d\) has kernel \(J_d\).  Under the perfect
catalectic pairings, its transpose is the summation map
\(\bigoplus_i\mathcal D_d(T_i)\to\operatorname{Sym}^dV\), whose image is
\(H_d\).  Hence \(\dim C_d=\dim H_d\).  The quotient \(C/R=S/I\) is the permanent
apolar algebra, whose degree-\(d\) dimension is \(\binom 7d^2\).  This
proves (N1) and (N2).

# theorem ext:shafiei-permanent-apolar-ideal

## statement

The following external result is used in full.

**Shafiei's quadratic-generation theorem.**  For the generic \(n\)-by-\(n\)
matrix \(X=(x_{ij})\), let \(Y=(y_{ij})\) be the dual matrix of
differential variables.  The apolar ideal of \(\operatorname{perm}_n(X)\)
is generated by the following quadrics:

1. \(y_{ij}^2\);
2. \(y_{ij}y_{ik}\) and \(y_{ij}y_{kj}\) for two variables in one row or
   one column; and
3. \(y_{ij}y_{k\ell}-y_{i\ell}y_{kj}\) for distinct rows \(i,k\) and
   distinct columns \(j,\ell\).

Source identifiers: paper_id `Shafiei-Apolarity-1212.0515`, theorem_id
`Theorem 2.13 (thm:main-generic-permanent)`, arXiv `1212.0515`, *Apolarity
for determinants and permanents of generic matrices*.

For \(P=\operatorname{perm}_7\), it follows that

\[
I=(I_2),\qquad R_3=C_1R_2,qquad R_4=C_1^2R_2,       \tag{Q1}
\]

and, in derivative language,

\[
E_2^{(1)}=E_3,\qquad E_3^{(1)}=E_4.                 \tag{Q2}
\]

## proof

This is Shafiei's theorem with \(n=7\).  Its terminology matches the present
one: an unacceptable monomial is a square or a product using a common row or
column, and the displayed rectangle determinants identify all partial
matchings with the same row and column subsets.  Thus the degree-\(d\)
quotient has one basis vector for every pair of \(d\)-subsets of rows and
columns, exactly the permanent apolar Hilbert function.  Hence there are no
additional generators.  Passing the identities \(I_{d+1}=S_1I_d\) modulo
\(J\) gives (Q1); apolar duality gives (Q2).

# theorem ext:bukh-multidimensional-kk

## statement

The following external combinatorial result is used.

**Bukh's multidimensional Kruskal--Katona theorem.**  If
\(\mathcal F\subseteq\binom Xr^m\) and
\(|\mathcal F|=\binom xr^m\) for a real \(x\ge r\), then the simultaneous
lower shadow, obtained by deleting one element in every coordinate, obeys

\[
|\partial\mathcal F|\ge\binom x{r-1}^m.
\]

Equality is possible only for
\(\mathcal F=\binom{Y_1}{r}\times\cdots\times\binom{Y_m}{r}\).
Moreover, the coordinatewise compression lemmas used in its proof replace a
finite family by a coordinatewise colex-monotone family of the same size and
with no larger simultaneous shadow.

Source identifiers: paper_id `Bukh-MDKK-1009.2375`, theorem_id `Theorem 1;
compression Lemmas 2 and 3`, arXiv `1009.2375v2`, *Multidimensional
Kruskal--Katona theorem*.

For \(m=2\), enumerate the \(r\)-subsets of \([7]\) in colex order and let
\(\kappa_r(a)\) be the ordinary lower-shadow size of the first \(a\) sets.
The compressed family is a Ferrers diagram with nonincreasing row heights
\(h_1\ge\cdots\ge h_M\), \(M=\binom7r\), and its simultaneous-shadow size
is

\[
\sum_{a=1}^{M}
  \bigl(\kappa_r(a)-\kappa_r(a-1)\bigr)\kappa_r(h_a). \tag{K1}
\]

## proof

The first paragraph is the complete theorem and the finite compression
consequence of Lemmas 2--3.  For (K1), group the shadow by the first colex
rank at which a new first-coordinate shadow element appears.  It appears
\(\kappa_r(a)-\kappa_r(a-1)\) times, and its second-coordinate fiber is the
ordinary shadow of a colex segment of height \(h_a\), of size
\(\kappa_r(h_a)\).  Summing the disjoint fibers gives (K1).

# lemma lem:exact-section-caps

## statement

For \(q\le49\), let \(C_d(q)\) be a universal upper bound for

\[
\dim\left(E_d\cap\sum_{i\in Q}\mathcal D_d(T_i)\right),
\qquad |Q|=q.
\]

Define \(\phi_d(b)\) to be the largest area \(\sum h_a\) of a Ferrers
diagram whose cost (K1) is at most \(b\).  Then

\[
C_1(q)=\min(7q,49),                                   \tag{K2}
\]

\[
C_d(q)=\min_{1\le a\le q}
\left((q-a)\binom7d+\phi_d(C_{d-1}(a))\right),
\quad 2\le d\le6,                                    \tag{K3}
\]

with the evident cap \(C_d(q)\le\binom7d^2\).  In particular,

\[
C_6(47)=37,qquad C_6(48)=44.                         \tag{K4}
\]

These assertions include Chow terms with repeated or dependent factors.

## proof

For subspaces \(E,A,B\) in one ambient space, projection modulo \(A\)
gives

\[
\dim(E\cap(A+B))\le \dim(E\cap A)+\dim B.            \tag{K5}
\]

Choose \(a\) of the \(q\) terms.  The derivative shadow of their
degree-\(d\) intersection with \(E_d\) lies in the corresponding
degree-\((d-1)\) intersection, of dimension at most \(C_{d-1}(a)\).
A generic row-column torus degenerates this subspace to a coordinate family
without increasing the shadow dimension.  Theorem
ext:bukh-multidimensional-kk and (K1) then bound the intersection dimension
by \(\phi_d(C_{d-1}(a))\).  Each remaining Chow term contributes at most
\(\binom7d\), proving (K3) after minimization.  A term with dependent
factors is a specialization of a product in seven formal independent
labels, so its derivative ranks and shadow ranks cannot increase.

The finite arithmetic in (K4) is exact.  The certificate
`scripts/n7_lower50_section_caps_audit.py` constructs
\(\kappa_d\) independently both from canonical binomial expansions and
from explicit colex segments, computes every \(\phi_d\) both by an
area-indexed and a budget-indexed dynamic program, and then evaluates (K3).
Its frozen payload `data/n7_lower50_section_caps_audit.json` records the full
table and \(C_6(47)=37\), \(C_6(48)=44\).  It also exhausts all 512 families
in the `n=3`, `r=2` coordinate control.

# proposition prop:factor-span-floors

## statement

If an identity \(P=\sum_{i=1}^{49}T_i\) exists, then

\[
\dim L_i\ge5,qquad \dim(L_i+L_j)\ge12\quad(i\ne j). \tag{F1}
\]

Consequently either every factor rank is six or seven, or exactly one term
has factor rank five and all other terms have factor rank seven.

## proof

Remove 48 terms and apply the complementary \((6,1)\)-catalectic to the
residual term.  Its permanent-side row space has dimension 49, and its
intersection with the selected row space is at most \(C_6(48)=44\).
Quotienting the selected rows therefore leaves residual catalectic rank at
least five.  Its column space is contained in \(L_i\), giving the first
inequality.

Removing 47 terms and using \(C_6(47)=37\) in the same way gives residual
rank at least \(49-37=12\) for \(T_i+T_j\).  Its column space lies in
\(L_i+L_j\), proving the second inequality.  Since every \(L_i\) has
dimension at most seven, (F1) gives the final alternatives.

# lemma lem:small-permanent-intersections

## statement

For every degree-seven Chow term \(T\), with actual factor span \(L\),

\[
\mathcal D_3(T)\cap E_3=0,qquad
\dim\bigl(\mathcal D_2(T)\cap E_2\bigr)\le3.          \tag{S1}
\]

## proof

Every nonzero element of \(E_3\) has at least nine essential variables.
Indeed, choose a nonzero coefficient in its basis of three-row,
three-column subpermanents and set all variables outside that block to
zero.  The restriction is a nonzero multiple of \(\operatorname{perm}_3\),
whose nine first derivatives have distinct row-column multidegrees.  Since
every element of \(\mathcal D_3(T)\) depends on at most the seven variables
in \(L\), the first intersection is zero.

Put \(W=\mathcal D_2(T)\cap E_2\).  Its collective derivative span is
contained in \(L\), hence has dimension at most seven.  Degenerate \(W\)
by a generic row-column one-parameter subgroup.  The limit is spanned by
\(\dim W\) coordinate two-by-two subpermanents, and upper semicontinuity
says that the union of their derivative variables has size at most seven.
Identify variables with edges of \(K_{7,7}\); the selected subpermanents
are four-cycles in a bipartite graph with at most seven edges.

Such a graph has at most three four-cycles.  If its maximum degree is at
least four, at most three edges lie off a maximum star, and every four-cycle
uses two of them, giving at most \(\binom32=3\).  If the maximum degree is
three, the four off-star edges have degree partition either \(3+1\), giving
at most three cycles through the center, or at most \(2+2\), giving at most
two through the center and at most one avoiding it.  If the maximum degree
is at most two, components are paths and cycles and seven edges allow at
most one four-cycle.  Therefore \(\dim W\le3\).

# lemma lem:universal-slope-ten

## statement

Let \(T\) be any nonzero product of seven linear forms, let
\(L=L(T)\), and put

\[
U_4=\mathcal D_4(T),\quad U_3=\mathcal D_3(T),\quad
F=\mathcal D_2(T),\quad u=\dim U_3=\dim U_4,
\]

\[
R_T=F\cap E_2,qquad \delta=35-u.
\]

For **every** quotient \(P_L:L\twoheadrightarrow D\) of rank \(d\), including
arbitrary quotient orientation, let

\[
\beta^+_{P_L}:U_4\longrightarrow D\otimes U_3,
\qquad
\beta^-_{P_L,R_T}:U_3\longrightarrow D\otimes(F/R_T) \tag{S4}
\]

be the projected first-polarization maps.  Then

\[
\operatorname{rank}\beta^+_{P_L}+
\operatorname{rank}\beta^-_{P_L,R_T}+\delta\ge10d.   \tag{S5}
\]

For factor rank seven, positive-\(d\) equality occurs only at \(d=7\).
For factor rank six, positive-\(d\) equality occurs only at \(d=6\) and
only for the support types \(s=1,2\) of Lemma
lem:rank-six-normal-forms.

## proof

Lemma lem:small-permanent-intersections gives
\(U_3\cap E_3=0\) and \(\dim R_T\le3\).

For factor rank six, use (S2) and a diagonal one-parameter subgroup that
selects one summand of the last factor.  The special product is

\[
x_1^2x_2x_3x_4x_5x_6.                                \tag{S6}
\]

The flat limits of the actual derivative spaces contain the derivative
spaces of (S6).  Carry the quotient kernel and \(R_T\) in their
Grassmannians, then use a second diagonal subgroup fixing (S6) to make both
limits coordinate.  Matrix rank is upper semicontinuous, so the coordinate
symbol ranks are lower bounds for the original symbols.  This argument
quantifies over every quotient and every at-most-three-dimensional
\(R_T\); it does not assume generic factors or a coordinate quotient.

The exhaustive coordinate rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrr}
d&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\ \hline
\min(\operatorname{rank}\beta^++\operatorname{rank}\beta^-)
 &0&22&33&37&41&44&48.
\end{array}                                            \tag{S7}
\]

For \(d\le4\), (S7) is already strict relative to \(10d\).  At \(d=5\),
the plus kernel lies in
\(U_4\cap\operatorname{Sym}^4(\ker P_L)\), of dimension at most one,
while the separate coordinate count gives \(\operatorname{rank}\beta^-
\ge19\).  Hence the left side of (S5) is at least

\[
(u-1)+19+(35-u)=53>50.
\]

At \(d=6\), full polarization is injective.  If a cubic is killed by
\(\beta^-\), all its first derivatives lie in \(R_T\subset E_2\), so it
lies in \(E_2^{(1)}=E_3\); Lemma lem:small-permanent-intersections makes
it zero.  Thus both maps are injective and the left side is \(u+35\ge60\),
with equality exactly when \(u=25\), namely \(s=1,2\).

For factor rank seven, the term is the squarefree monomial in seven
variables.  The corresponding exact row is

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}
d&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\ \hline
\min(\operatorname{rank}\beta^++\operatorname{rank}\beta^-)
 &0&32&49&56&57&64&67&69.
\end{array}                                            \tag{S8}
\]

It is strict for \(1\le d\le6\); at \(d=7\), full injectivity gives
\(35+35=70\), which is equality in (S5).

For factor rank five, diagonal degeneration leaves only the exponent
partitions \((3,1,1,1,1)\) and \((2,2,1,1,1)\).  Their exact combined
rows are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
d&0&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
(3,1,1,1,1)&0&12&17&21&24&27\\
(2,2,1,1,1)&0&15&22&24&30&34.
\end{array}                                            \tag{S9}
\]

The \(d=1\) rows are already strict.  At \(d=2\), the plus kernel lies in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^4(\ker P_L)\), whose dimension is 15, so

\[
\operatorname{rank}\beta^++\delta
\ge (u-15)+(35-u)=20.
\]

At \(d=3\), the analogous kernel dimension is five, giving 30.  At
\(d=4\), it is one and the separate coordinate count gives
\(\operatorname{rank}\beta^-\ge9\), for a total at least \(34+9>40\).
At full rank, both symbols are injective and the smallest possible \(u\) is
15, giving \(u+35\ge50\).  Thus a rank-five full quotient with \(u=15\)
is an additional positive-increment equality type.  It does not survive the
global endpoint argument: the factor-span floors make that term unique, and
the remaining ambient dimension 44 is not a sum of rank-seven equality
increments in \(\{0,7\}\).

Finally consider factor ranks \(1,2,3,4\).  Since
\(u\le\dim\operatorname{Sym}^3L\), the defect floors are respectively
\(34,31,25,15\).  They settle every quotient for ranks at most three except
the full rank-three row; full injectivity gives \(u+35>30\) there.  For
rank four, the defect settles \(d=1\).  At \(d=2\), if \(u\ge5\), the
five-dimensional plus-kernel bound gives
\(\operatorname{rank}\beta^++\delta\ge30\); if \(u<5\), the defect alone
is at least31.  At \(d=3\), the one-dimensional plus-kernel bound gives at
least34.  At the full quotient, a positive-monomial degeneration gives
\(u\ge8\), and full injectivity gives \(u+35\ge43>40\).  This proves (S5)
for every actual factor rank.

The finite rows (S7)--(S9) contain respectively 44,608, 199,936, and a
smaller number of coordinate cases, well below the symbolic capacity gate.
They were regenerated by
`scripts/n7_slope10_coordinate_symbol_table.py` and
independently by
`scripts/n7_slope10_coordinate_symbol_table_independent.py`;
both exact replays passed on 2026-08-22.  A separate 4,128-case exact
finite-field adversarial test of arbitrary quotient orientations and
quadratic subspaces also found no violation, but that diagnostic is not
used in the characteristic-zero proof.

# lemma lem:rectangular-sylvester

## statement

For an identity \(P=\sum_{i=1}^{49}T_i\), put
\(u_i=\dim\mathcal D_3(T_i)=\dim\mathcal D_4(T_i)\) and
\(\delta_i=35-u_i\).  Then

\[
h_3+h_4\le490-\sum_i\delta_i.                         \tag{G1}
\]

## proof

Factor each middle catalectic of \(T_i\) through a space of dimension
\(u_i\).  Stacking these factorizations gives maps \(B\) and \(A\) with
inner space of dimension \(\sum_i u_i\), with

\[
\operatorname{rank}A=\dim H_3,\quad
\operatorname{rank}B=\dim H_4,\quad
\operatorname{rank}(AB)=\dim E_3=1225.
\]

Sylvester's rank inequality yields

\[
\dim H_3+\dim H_4\le\sum_i u_i+1225.
\]

Subtracting \(2\cdot1225\), and using
\(\sum_i u_i=49\cdot35-\sum_i\delta_i\), proves (G1).

# proposition prop:slope-endpoint-classification

## statement

Every hypothetical 49-term identity has exactly one of the following two
factor packets.

1. **Case A.**  All 49 terms have factor rank seven, and their factor
   planes represent a simple rank-seven 7-multilinear matroid:
   \[
   \dim\left(\sum_{i\in Q}L_i\right)=7r_{\mathcal M}(Q)
   \quad\text{for every }Q.
   \]
2. **Case B.**  Exactly seven terms have factor rank six and support type
   \(s=1\) or \(s=2\).  Their planes are mutually direct and have
   42-dimensional sum \(A\).  The remaining 42 terms have rank seven and
   are graph complements to \(A\).  Every pair of graph planes spans at
   least twelve dimensions.

## proof

Order the terms, put \(W_i=\sum_{j\le i}L_j\), and set
\(d_i=\dim(W_i/W_{i-1})\).  Since \(E_1=V\) lies in the sum of the factor
spans, \(\sum_i d_i=49\).

By (Q2), the global polarizations

\[
H_4\longrightarrow V\otimes(H_3/E_3),\qquad
H_3\longrightarrow V\otimes(H_2/E_2)
\]

have kernels exactly \(E_4\) and \(E_3\), hence ranks \(h_4\) and \(h_3\).
Filter their images by the first tensor factor \(W_i\).  Projecting the
\(i\)-th increment to \(W_i/W_{i-1}\) kills all earlier terms.  Lemma
lem:small-permanent-intersections identifies the surviving quotients with
the two local symbols of Lemma lem:universal-slope-ten.  Therefore

\[
h_3+h_4\ge\sum_i(10d_i-\delta_i)
=490-\sum_i\delta_i.                                  \tag{G2}
\]

Equations (G1) and (G2) are equalities, as is every local inequality used.
If a rank-five term exists, Proposition prop:factor-span-floors makes it
unique.  Put it first, so its quotient has rank five.  The other 44 ambient
dimensions would have to be a sum of rank-seven equality increments, each
zero or seven, which is impossible.

All terms thus have rank six or seven.  Since the argument works in every
ordering, place each rank-six term first in turn.  Its increment is six, so
it must have support type \(s=1,2\).  Now order all rank-six terms first.
For these types the zero-increment row is strict because \(\delta=10\),
and the only positive equality increment is six.  Hence their planes are
mutually direct.  If there are \(m\) such terms, then \(6m\le49\), and the
remaining ambient dimension \(49-6m\) is a sum of zero-or-seven rank-seven
increments.  Thus \(m\equiv0\pmod7\) and \(m\le8\), so \(m=0\) or \(m=7\).

When \(m=0\), every subset span has dimension divisible by seven.  The
pairwise floor (F1) makes the resulting multilinear matroid simple, giving
Case A.  When \(m=7\), the rank-six planes are mutually direct.  Put any
rank-seven term first and the seven rank-six terms next; equality forces
that rank-seven plane to complement their 42-dimensional sum.  This holds
for all 42 rank-seven terms, and (F1) supplies their pairwise span floor,
giving Case B.

# lemma lem:correct-quadratic-defect-duality

## statement

For every term \(T_i\), the restriction

\[
\rho_i:I_2\longrightarrow(A_i)_2
\]

has

\[
\operatorname{coker}(\rho_i)^*
\simeq \mathcal D_2(T_i)\cap E_2,qquad
\dim\operatorname{coker}(\rho_i)\le3.                 \tag{L1}
\]

For a pair, the dual cokernel is

\[
\operatorname{coker}\!\left(I_2\to(A_i)_2\oplus(A_j)_2\right)^*
\simeq E_2\cap\bigl(\mathcal D_2(T_i)+\mathcal D_2(T_j)\bigr). \tag{L2}
\]

Neither restriction need be onto, even when both terms have seven
independent factors and, in (L2), their factor planes are disjoint.

## proof

Use the Gorenstein pairing
\((A_i)_2\times(A_i)_5\to(A_i)_7\simeq k\).  A functional on
\((A_i)_2\) annihilating the image of \(I_2\) is represented by
\(p\in(A_i)_5\).  The quadratic form \(p\mathbin{\lrcorner}T_i\) lies in
\(\mathcal D_2(T_i)\) and is annihilated by \(I_2\).  Since \(I=(I_2)\),
the degree-two inverse system is exactly \(E_2\).  This proves the first
isomorphism, and Lemma lem:small-permanent-intersections gives its
dimension bound.  The same pairing on a direct sum proves (L2).

For failure of local surjectivity, take

\[
T=x_{11}x_{12}x_{21}x_{22}x_{33}x_{44}x_{55}.
\]

Its quadratic derivative space contains
\(x_{11}x_{22}+x_{12}x_{21}\), the coordinate two-by-two subpermanent on
rows 1,2 and columns 1,2.  Thus the intersection in (L1) is nonzero.

There is an even sharper pair failure.  Put

\[
T_\pm=\prod_{c=1}^7(x_{1c}\pm x_{2c}),\qquad
\ell_c=x_{1c}+x_{2c},\quad m_c=x_{1c}-x_{2c}.
\]

The factor planes \(L_+\) and \(L_-\) are disjoint, and each individual
intersection in (L1) is zero.  Nevertheless, for every \(a<b\),

\[
\ell_a\ell_b-m_am_b
=2(x_{1a}x_{2b}+x_{2a}x_{1b})\in E_2.
\]

These 21 forms are independent.  Hence the pair defect in (L2) can have
dimension at least 21 despite disjoint factor planes and zero individual
defects.

# lemma lem:corrected-direct-basis-middle-projection

## statement

Suppose a subpacket \(B\) gives a direct factor decomposition
\(V=\bigoplus_{i\in B}L_i\).  Then, for \(d=3,4\),

\[
R_d\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in B}(A_i)_d          \tag{L3}
\]

is onto.  This conclusion uses the full middle symbols, not the false
quadratic-surjectivity claim.  Consequently

\[
\sum_{i\in B}\dim(A_i)_3\le245.                      \tag{L4}
\]

If equality holds in (L4), both maps (L3) are isomorphisms.

## proof

For \(d=3,4\), correctly graded Gorenstein duality gives

\[
\operatorname{coker}\left(R_d\to\bigoplus_{i\in B}(A_i)_d\right)^*
\simeq
\left\{(F_i):F_i\in\mathcal D_d(T_i),\
\sum_{i\in B}F_i\in E_d\right\}.                    \tag{L5}
\]

Order the basis terms first.  Their successive factor-span quotients are
their full \(L_i\).  On \(\mathcal D_3(T_i)\), the full minus symbol is
injective: a kernel cubic has all derivatives in
\(\mathcal D_2(T_i)\cap E_2\), hence lies in
\(E_2^{(1)}=E_3\), and Lemma lem:small-permanent-intersections makes it
zero.  On \(\mathcal D_4(T_i)\), full polarization is injective, and its
second factor remains injective modulo \(E_3\) because
\(\mathcal D_3(T_i)\cap E_3=0\).

The two global quotient symbols are block triangular with these injective
diagonal blocks.  Thus their restrictions to
\(\bigoplus_{i\in B}\mathcal D_3(T_i)\) and
\(\bigoplus_{i\in B}\mathcal D_4(T_i)\) are injective.  Their kernels are,
respectively, the tuples whose sums lie in \(E_3\) and \(E_4\), by (Q2).
Formula (L5) therefore makes both restriction maps onto.

Let \(M_B=\sum_{i\in B}\dim(A_i)_3\); Gorenstein symmetry gives the same
degree-four total.  Hence \(\dim R_3,\dim R_4\ge M_B\).  Together with
(N2) and (G1), this gives (L4), and equality makes the surjections
isomorphisms.

# lemma lem:corrected-exclude-endpoint-A

## statement

Case A of Proposition prop:slope-endpoint-classification is impossible.

## proof

Choose a matroid basis \(L_1,\ldots,L_7\).  The seven local Boolean middle
blocks have total dimension \(7\cdot35=245\).  Lemma
lem:corrected-direct-basis-middle-projection and the global bound give

\[
\dim R_3=\dim R_4=245,                                \tag{A1}
\]

and projection to the basis blocks is an isomorphism in both degrees.

Fix a nonbasis term \(t\).  Relative to
\(V=L_1\oplus\cdots\oplus L_7\), let
\(P_{tj}:(A_j)_1\to(A_t)_1\) be the restriction blocks.  The multilinear
matroid law applied after omitting one basis plane makes every \(P_{tj}\)
either zero or invertible.  The invertible blocks are precisely the basis
indices in the fundamental circuit of \(t\), and simplicity makes that
circuit contain at least two basis indices.

For each basis index \(i\) and cubic \(u\in(A_i)_3\), let its unique lift
through the degree-three isomorphism (A1) have \(t\)-component
\(\phi_{ti}(u)\).  Choose a circuit index \(j\ne i\), and multiply by a
linear codeword supported only at basis block \(j\).  Every basis component
of the resulting quartic is zero; the degree-four isomorphism in (A1)
therefore makes the entire relation zero.  At term \(t\),

\[
(P_{tj}x)\phi_{ti}(u)=0\quad\text{for all }x,u.
\]

As \(P_{tj}\) is onto, all linear forms annihilate \(\phi_{ti}(u)\).  In
the Boolean complete intersection
\(A_t=k[e_1,\ldots,e_7]/(e_1^2,\ldots,e_7^2)\), no nonzero cubic is
annihilated by every linear form: multiply a nonzero squarefree monomial
coefficient by a missing variable.  Hence every \(\phi_{ti}\) is zero, and
the projection of \(R_3\) to term \(t\) is zero.  Correctly graded duality
gives

\[
\operatorname{coker}\bigl(R_3\to(A_t)_3\bigr)^*
\simeq\mathcal D_3(T_t)\cap E_3=0.
\]

The same projection is therefore onto, a contradiction.

# lemma lem:corrected-exclude-endpoint-B

## statement

Case B of Proposition prop:slope-endpoint-classification is impossible.

## proof

Let the seven rank-six planes have direct sum \(A\), and choose one
rank-seven graph complement \(L_0\).  These eight planes give a direct
factor decomposition of \(V\).  Their total local middle dimension is

\[
7\cdot25+35=210.                                      \tag{B1}
\]

Lemma lem:corrected-direct-basis-middle-projection gives both middle
dimensions at least 210.  The packet-specific Sylvester bound is

\[
h_3+h_4\le7\cdot25+42\cdot35-1225=420.               \tag{B2}
\]

By (N2), both dimensions equal 210 and the restrictions of \(R_3,R_4\)
to the eight basis blocks are isomorphisms.

Take a second graph plane \(L_t\), the graph of
\(N_t:L_0\to A\).  Since \(\dim(L_t+L_0)\ge12\),
\(\operatorname{rank}N_t\ge5\).

For a cubic \(u\) in one basis block \(b\), let \(r_b(u)\in R_3\) be its
unique lift supported only at \(b\) on the basis, and write
\(\phi_{tb}(u)=r_b(u)|_t\).  If \(b\ne c\), multiply by a linear codeword
supported only at basis block \(c\).  Its product has zero component on
every basis block, so the degree-four basis isomorphism gives

\[
(P_{tc}x)\phi_{tb}(u)=0.                              \tag{B3}
\]

If \(b\) is one of the seven rank-six blocks, take \(c=0\).  Projection
from the graph \(L_t\) to \(L_0\) is an isomorphism, so \(P_{t0}\) is
invertible.  The Boolean algebra \(A_t\) has no socle in degree three;
(B3) therefore gives \(\phi_{tb}=0\).

For \(b=0\), let

\[
W=\sum_{c=1}^7\operatorname{im}P_{tc}=\operatorname{im}N_t^*.
\]

Then \(\dim W\ge5\), and (B3) gives \(W\phi_{t0}(u)=0\).  In the Boolean
algebra \(A_t=k[e_1,\ldots,e_7]/(e_i^2)\), put \(B=A_t/(W)\).  If
\(\dim B_1\le1\), then \(B_2=0\).  Otherwise choose two independent images
\(x,y\) among the images of the \(e_i\).  They satisfy \(x^2=y^2=0\).  Every
other image is \(a x+b y\), and its square is \(2abxy\).  In characteristic
zero, either one such \(ab\) is nonzero and \(xy=0\), or all seven images lie
on the two coordinate directions, in which case every degree at least three
vanishes.  Thus \(B_4=0\) in all cases.  Hence

\[
W(A_t)_3=(A_t)_4.                                    \tag{B4}
\]

If \(q\in(A_t)_3\) satisfies \(Wq=0\), associativity makes it orthogonal
to \(W(A_t)_3=(A_t)_4\) under the perfect degree-\((3,4)\) Gorenstein
pairing.  Thus \(q=0\), so \(\phi_{t0}=0\) as well.

Every basis lift has zero \(t\)-component, hence \(R_3\to(A_t)_3\) is the
zero map.  Correctly graded duality and Lemma
lem:small-permanent-intersections say its cokernel dual is
\(\mathcal D_3(T_t)\cap E_3=0\), so it is onto.  This contradiction
excludes Case B.

# theorem thm:ordinary-lower-50

## statement

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_7)\ge50.}
\]

## proof

Suppose a decomposition with at most 49 nonzero terms exists.  If necessary,
replace a term \(T\) by \(\lambda T+(1-\lambda)T\), with
\(\lambda\ne0,1\), absorbing each scalar into one factor.  Repeating gives
an identity with exactly 49 nonzero terms; repeated terms are allowed.

Proposition prop:slope-endpoint-classification puts this packet in Case A
or Case B.  Lemma lem:corrected-exclude-endpoint-A excludes Case A, while
Lemma lem:corrected-exclude-endpoint-B excludes Case B.  Both exhaustive
alternatives are impossible.  Notice that the proof never uses local or
pairwise quadratic surjectivity; Lemma
lem:correct-quadratic-defect-duality shows those tempting statements are
false.

# lemma lem:permanent-apolar-centroid

## statement

Let (A_P) be the apolar algebra of (P=\operatorname{perm}_7).  The
centroid of its multiplication in degrees one and two,

\[
 \operatorname{Cent}(P)=\{\theta\in\operatorname{End}((A_P)_1):
 \theta(a)b=a\theta(b)\text{ for all }a,b\in(A_P)_1\},
\]

consists only of scalar multiples of the identity.  Consequently the
permanent has no nontrivial Sebastiani--Thom decomposition
(P=G(U)+H(W)) with (V=U\oplus W) and both summands nonzero.

## proof

Write (y_{ij}) for the class of the dual matrix variable.  Products with a
common row or column vanish.  For distinct rows and columns,

\[
 y_{ij}y_{k\ell}=y_{i\ell}y_{kj}\ne0,                \tag{C1}
\]

and these classes, indexed by an unordered row pair and an unordered column
pair, form a basis of ((A_P)_2).

Let (\theta) lie in the centroid.  Comparing
(\theta(y_{ij})y_{i\ell}=y_{ij}\theta(y_{i\ell})), first on rectangle
classes whose column pair omits (j), gives

\[
 \theta(y_{ij})\in
 \operatorname{span}\{y_{iq}:q=1,\ldots,7\}
 +\operatorname{span}\{y_{pj}:p=1,\ldots,7\}.         \tag{C2}
\]

If (q\ne j), choose (k\ne i) and
(\ell\notin\{j,q\}).  In
(\theta(y_{ij})y_{k\ell}=y_{ij}\theta(y_{k\ell})), the coefficient of
the rectangle with rows (\{i,k\}) and columns (\{q,\ell\}) kills the
coefficient of (y_{iq}) in (\theta(y_{ij})).  The transposed argument
kills every (y_{pj}), (p\ne i).  Thus (\theta) is diagonal.  Applying
the centroid identity to variables in distinct rows and columns makes their
diagonal entries equal.  The graph on the 49 positions joining positions in
distinct rows and columns is connected, so (\theta) is scalar.

If (P=G(U)+H(W)), projection (V^*\to U^*) is a nonzero, nonidentity
idempotent in this centroid: cross products between (U^*) and (W^*)
annihilate (P).  This contradicts the first assertion.

# proposition prop:simple-N50-exclusion

## statement

There is no 50-term identity for the permanent in which every term has seven
independent factors and the 50 factor planes represent a simple rank-seven
7-multilinear matroid.

## proof

Suppose such an identity exists.  Choose a matroid basis
(B=\{1,\ldots,7\}), so
(V=L_1\oplus\cdots\oplus L_7).  For (t\notin B), let
(S_t\subseteq B) be the basis support of its fundamental circuit.  Relative
to this direct sum, the degree-one restriction block

\[
 P_{tc}:(A_c)_1\longrightarrow(A_t)_1
\]

is zero for (c\notin S_t) and invertible for (c\in S_t); simplicity
gives (|S_t|\ge2).

Let (K_d) be the kernel of the restriction from (R_d) to the seven basis
blocks.  Lemma lem:corrected-direct-basis-middle-projection makes those
restrictions onto in degrees three and four.  The rectangular Sylvester
bound with 50 rank-seven terms gives

\[
 \dim R_3+\dim R_4\le50\binom73-\binom73^2=525,
\]

and hence

\[
 \dim K_3+\dim K_4\le35.                             \tag{C3}
\]

We claim that (K_4\to(A_t)_4) is onto for every (t\notin B).  Indeed,
the individual restriction (R_3\to(A_t)_3) is onto because its dual
cokernel is
(\mathcal D_3(T_t)\cap E_3=0).  Choose linear sections of the seven basis
projections in degree three.  An arbitrary (t)-cubic is therefore a sum of
the (t)-components of seven basis-supported lifts and of one element of
(K_3).  For a lift supported at (c), choose
(j\in S_t\setminus\{c\}), using any (j\in S_t) if (c\notin S_t), and
multiply by a degree-one codeword supported at (j).  The product lies in
(K_4), while its (t)-component ranges through
((A_t)_1) times the given cubic component.  Multiplying a (K_3)-element
by a codeword at any (j\in S_t) does the same for its component.  Since
((A_t)_1(A_t)_3=(A_t)_4), the claim follows.

Thus (dim K_4\ge35).  Equation (C3) now forces

\[
 K_3=0,qquad \dim K_4=35,qquad
 K_4\xrightarrow{\sim}(A_t)_4\quad(t\notin B).        \tag{C4}
\]

In particular (R_3\to\bigoplus_{b\in B}(A_b)_3) is an isomorphism.  Let
(r_b(u)) be the cubic lift supported at basis block (b), and put

\[
 \Phi_{tb}(u)=r_b(u)|_t.
\]

If (b\in S_t), basis exchange replaces (b) by (t).  Applying Lemma
lem:corrected-direct-basis-middle-projection to the exchanged basis, whose
cubic total is again 245, shows that

\[
 \Phi_{tb}:(A_b)_3\xrightarrow{\sim}(A_t)_3.          \tag{C5}
\]

Fix two nonbasis labels (0,t).  Evaluation in (C4) defines an isomorphism

\[
 \psi_t=(\operatorname{ev}_t)(\operatorname{ev}_0)^{-1}:
 (A_0)_4\xrightarrow{\sim}(A_t)_4.                   \tag{C6}
\]

For (c\in S_0), choose (b\in S_0\setminus\{c\}).  If (x) is a
linear codeword supported at (c), then (xr_b(u)\in K_4).  Its component
at 0 can be chosen nonzero by (C5).  Injectivity of evaluation at (t) in
(C4) shows that its (t)-component cannot vanish, so (c\in S_t).  Reversing
0 and (t) proves

\[
 S_t=S\quad\text{for every }t\notin B                 \tag{C7}
\]

for one fixed subset (S\subseteq B).

First suppose (S=B).  Normalize the invertible restriction maps by

\[
 M_{tc}=P_{tc}P_{0c}^{-1},\qquad
 N_{tb}=\Phi_{tb}\Phi_{0b}^{-1}.
\]

Evaluating (xr_b(u)\in K_4) at 0 and (t), for (c\ne b), gives

\[
 \psi_t(av)=M_{tc}(a)N_{tb}(v)                       \tag{C8}
\]

for all (a\in(A_0)_1), (v\in(A_0)_3).  The Boolean multiplication
((A_t)_1\times(A_t)_3\to(A_t)_4) has zero radical in each argument.
Comparing (C8) for two values of (b), using a third value of (c), makes
all (N_{tb}) equal.  Comparing two values of (c), using a third value of
(b), makes all (M_{tc}) equal to one invertible map (M_t).  Hence the
complete degree-one restrictions satisfy (P_t=M_tP_0).  They have the same
kernel, so (L_t=L_0), contrary to simplicity.

It remains that (S\subsetneq B).  Equation (C7) implies
(L_t\subseteq V_S:=\bigoplus_{c\in S}L_c) for every nonbasis label.  The
actual identity then has the form

\[
 P=F_S+H,qquad
 F_S\in\operatorname{Sym}^7V_S,quad
 H=\sum_{c\notin S}T_c\in\operatorname{Sym}^7V_{S^c}. \tag{C9}
\]

Both summands are nonzero.  The terms in (H) lie in distinct direct
variable blocks, so they cannot cancel.  If (F_S=0), then (P) would lie
in the symmetric algebra on a proper subspace, contrary to conciseness of
the permanent (its 49 first derivatives have distinct missing-row and
missing-column supports).  Thus (C9) is a nontrivial Sebastiani--Thom
decomposition, contradicting Lemma lem:permanent-apolar-centroid.

# proposition prop:post-49-slope-slack

## statement

After the corrected middle-degree repair proves lower 50, the scalar slope
method still does not prove exact rank 64.  For a
hypothetical \(N\)-term identity, \(N\ge50\), its local surplus over (S5)
obeys only

\[
\sum_i\sigma_i\le35(N-49).                            \tag{P1}
\]

For rank-seven terms the exact surplus row at increments \(d=0,\ldots,7\)
is

\[
(0,22,29,26,17,14,7,0).                              \tag{P2}
\]

Already at \(N=50\), (P1) permits the realizable increment profile
\(7^6+6+1\).  More strongly, there is a characteristic-zero representable
packet for which every ordering has that profile and, after a generic
placement relative to the permanent, the actual orientation-sensitive local
symbols have total surplus exactly \(35\).  Thus neither dimension arithmetic
nor the two separate middle symbols, even in every ordering, can continue the
proof without cross-term compatibility.

## proof

Subtract the baseline \(10d_i-\delta_i\) from the exact local rows and sum
as in Proposition prop:slope-endpoint-classification.  The Sylvester upper
bound now has 35 additional units for each term beyond 49, giving (P1) and
(P2).

For the stronger countermodel, write

\[
V=V_0\oplus\cdots\oplus V_6,
\qquad(\dim V_0,\ldots,\dim V_6)=(6,7,7,7,7,7,8).
\]

In each \(V_r\), choose 50 projective points representing the uniform
matroid \(U_{\dim V_r,50}\), and for each label \(t\) let \(L_t\) be the
direct sum of the seven points with label \(t\).  Then for every subset
\(S\) of labels,

\[
\dim\sum_{t\in S}L_t
=\min(|S|,6)+5\min(|S|,7)+\min(|S|,8).
\]

Consequently every ordering has increments \(7^6,6,1,0,\ldots,0\).
Choose one Chow factor on each of the seven component lines of \(L_t\).
A generic simultaneous element of \(\mathrm{GL}(V)\) makes
\(\mathcal D_2(T_t)\cap E_2=0\) for all 50 labels: for one independent
term the failure condition is proper closed (placing all seven factors in
one matrix row exhibits an empty intersection), and finitely many nonempty
open conditions meet.

For the seventh positive increment, the quotient kernel is one actual
factor direction.  Both Boolean middle symbols are injective, of ranks 35
and 35, so its surplus is \(70-60=10\).  For the eighth increment, the
rank-one quotient is supported on one actual factor.  The two directional
derivative ranks are

\[
\binom63=20,\qquad \binom62=15,
\]

so its surplus is \(35-10=25\).  Full and zero increments have zero
surplus.  Thus every ordering has total surplus \(10+25=35\), exactly the
right side of (P1) at \(N=50\).  This packet is a countermodel to the
proposed local-symbol exclusion, not a Chow decomposition of the permanent.

# lemma lem:complementary-koszul-residual

## statement

For \(m=2,3\), let \(K_m(F)\) be the first Koszul flattening built from the
degree-\(m\) derivative space of a degree-seven form.  Its rank on the
permanent is

\[
A_m=49\binom7m^2-\binom7{m+1}^2,                     \tag{R1}
\]

and its rank on one arbitrary Chow term is at most

\[
B_m=49\binom7m-\binom7{m+1}.                         \tag{R2}
\]

If \(Q\) is any sum of selected terms and

\[
b=\dim\bigl(E_{7-m}\cap\mathcal D_{7-m}(Q)\bigr),
\]

then

\[
\operatorname{rank}K_m(P-Q)\ge A_m-49b.              \tag{R3}
\]

## proof

For a derivative subspace \(G\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^mV\), the first
Koszul differential has rank

\[
49\dim G-\dim G^{(1)},                                \tag{R4}
\]

because its kernel is the first prolongation \(G^{(1)}\).  For
\(G=E_m\), quadratic generation gives \(G^{(1)}=E_{m+1}\), proving (R1).
An independent-factor Chow term gives equality in (R2) by the Boolean
support calculation; every dependent-factor term is its linear
specialization, so rank semicontinuity proves the universal cap.

For (R3), put \(H_j=\mathcal D_j(Q)\),
\(a=\dim(E_m\cap H_m)\), and
\(h=\operatorname{rank}C_{7-m,m}(Q)\).  The horizontal/vertical
double-quotient rank inequality for the two catalectics gives

\[
\dim\mathcal D_m(P-Q)\ge\binom7m^2+h-a-b.            \tag{R5}
\]

Moreover,

\[
\dim(E_m+H_m)^{(1)}
\le\binom7{m+1}^2+49(h-a),                            \tag{R6}
\]

because first derivatives modulo \(E_m\) inject into
\((E_m+H_m)/E_m\otimes V^*\).  Apply (R4) to
\(\mathcal D_m(P-Q)\subseteq E_m+H_m\), substitute (R5)--(R6), and cancel
\(h-a\).  The result is (R3).

# theorem thm:audited-ordinary-lower-49

## statement

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,

\[
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_7)\ge49.
\]

## proof

The middle catalectic first gives the elementary lower bound
\(1225/35=35\), so twenty terms may be selected from any contrary
decomposition.  The exact recurrence (K3), independently replayed in the
certificate cited in Lemma lem:exact-section-caps, gives

\[
C_2(5)=85,\quad C_3(20)=589,\quad C_4(20)=341,
\]

\[
C_4(42)=1111,\quad C_5(46)=405,\quad C_6(46)=33.      \tag{R7}
\]

For twenty selected terms, use (R3) with \(m=3\) and \(b\le341\):

\[
\operatorname{rank}K_3(P-Q)
\ge58800-49\cdot341=42091>25\cdot1680.
\]

By (R2), at least 26 residual terms are required, proving rank at least46.
Now select 46 terms.  With \(m=2\) and \(b\le405\),

\ge20384-49\cdot405=539>0,

# theorem thm:current-ordinary-interval

## statement

Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero,

\[
50\le\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_7)\le64.
\]

## proof

The lower bound is Theorem thm:ordinary-lower-50.  For the upper bound use
Glynn's identity

\[
\operatorname{perm}_7(X)=2^{-6}
\sum_{\substack{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^7\\\varepsilon_1=1}}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^7\varepsilon_i\right)
\prod_{j=1}^7\left(\sum_{i=1}^7\varepsilon_i x_{ij}\right).
\]

There are \(2^6=64\) products.  Walsh orthogonality shows that the
coefficient of a row-multilinear monomial is one precisely when its column
choices form a permutation and is zero otherwise, proving the identity.
