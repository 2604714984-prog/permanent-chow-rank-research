## 4. Squarefree cubic spaces contain no binary cubic

### Lemma 4.1

Let

\[
C
=
\operatorname{span}
\{x_ix_jx_k:i<j<k\}
\subseteq
\operatorname{Sym}^3\operatorname{span}\{x_1,\ldots,x_6\}.
\]

Then no nonzero element of `C` depends on at most two linear forms.

### Proof

Suppose

\[
0\ne q
=a z^3+3bz^2w+3czw^2+dw^3
\]

with `z,w` independent. Write the coefficient pair of the coordinate `x_i` in `(z,w)` as

\[
p_i=(r_i,s_i).
\]

Because `q` is squarefree in the `x_i`, one has

\[
\partial_{x_i}^2q=0.
\]

Equivalently, every nonzero `p_i` is a common projective zero of

\[
A(r,s)=ar^2+2brs+cs^2,
\]

\[
B(r,s)=br^2+2crs+ds^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The pairs `p_i` span a two-dimensional space because `z,w` are independent, so (4.1) has at least two distinct projective common zeros. Two binary quadratics with two distinct common zeros have a common quadratic factor and are proportional. The binary cubic then has identically rank-one Hessian, which forces it to be a cube of one linear form. In that case the common zero of `A,B` is a single double point, contradicting the existence of two distinct zeros. ∎

This is the same squarefree-binary obstruction used in the corrected small-`n` coupling analysis; no finite-field inference is involved.

## 5. Cubic relations factor through the quadratic relation kernel

### Lemma 5.1 — `k`-relation derivative factorization

Let quadratic and cubic spaces satisfy

\[
\partial C_i\subseteq G_i.
\]

If the quadratic relation kernel has dimension at most `k`, then every component of a cubic relation

\[
c_1+\cdots+c_4=0,
\qquad
c_i\in C_i,
\]

depends on at most `k` linear forms.

### Proof

Differentiation defines a linear map

\[
V^*
\longrightarrow
\ker
\left(
\bigoplus_iG_i\longrightarrow U
\right),
\qquad
\xi\longmapsto(\partial_\xi c_i)_i.
\]

Its rank is at most `k`. Every direction in its kernel annihilates every `c_i`. Therefore the `c_i` descend to the quotient of `V` by a codimension-`k` subspace and depend on at most `k` linear forms. ∎

For `k=1`, every nonzero component is a pure cube. For `k=2`, every nonzero component is a binary cubic.
