# N6-117: block-diagonal (K_{3,2}) graph rigidity

**Status.** `PURE_BLOCK_DIAGONAL_K32_GRAPH_RIGIDITY`,
`EXACT_QQ_COMMUTATOR_REPLAY`, and `BOUNDED_F3_DIAGNOSTIC`. The base field is
algebraically closed of characteristic zero.

N6-115 proves that the common-(A_3) harmonic product family has cross rank
six but loses three directions on each Chow block. This note shows that there
is no hidden row twist inside the entire graph family that preserves the same
(2+2) column matching.

## 1. Statement

Let (A=k^3) and decompose (B=k^4=P\oplus Q), where

\[
 P=\langle e_0,e_1\rangle,\qquad Q=\langle e_2,e_3\rangle.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For invertible (S,R\in\operatorname{End}(A)), define

\[
 T(a\otimes e_0)=Sa\otimes e_2,
 \qquad
 T(a\otimes e_1)=Ra\otimes e_3,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and put

\[
 L=\operatorname{graph}(T),\qquad M=\operatorname{graph}(-T).
\tag{1.3}
\]

### Theorem 1.1

If the full (E_{34}=S_0(A)\otimes S_0(B)) cross rank of (L,M) is at
most six, then (S) and (R) are scalar. Consequently (1.3) is one of the
common-(A_3) product pairs of N6-115, and its twelve-dimensional cross-free
kernel has block projection rank at most nine.

## 2. The three column-pair blocks

Write (P_A=S_0(A)), of dimension three. Split the cross evaluations into
the mixed part, where the two source columns are different, and the same-class
part. Their supports use disjoint column edges: the mixed part uses
(01,03,12,23), while the same-class part uses (02,13). Hence their image
dimensions add.

The mixed image already has dimension at least six. It is enough to project
to the (01) and (03) column edges. If a pair
((B,C)\in P_A^2) annihilates that projection, evaluation on
(L_0(a)\times M_1(b)) and (L_1(a)\times M_0(b)) gives, respectively,

\[
 B=CR,\qquad B=-R^{\mathsf T}C.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Transposing the first identity, using the symmetry of (B,C), also gives
(B=R^{\mathsf T}C). Characteristic zero now forces (B=0), and the
invertibility of (R) forces (C=0). The dual annihilator is zero, so the
projection is the full six-dimensional (P_A^2).

For the same-class column pairs (02) and (13), the cross terms contain the
commutator maps

\[
 B\longmapsto S^{\mathsf T}B-BS,
 \qquad
 B\longmapsto R^{\mathsf T}B-BR,
 \qquad B\in P_A.
\tag{2.2}
\]

They lie in the two disjoint same-class weights. Since the mixed image already
has dimension at least six, full cross rank at most six forces both maps in
(2.2) to vanish identically.

### Lemma 2.1

If (X^{\mathsf T}B=BX) for every (B\in S_0(k^3)), then (X) is scalar.

### Proof

Apply the equation to the three symmetric matrix units
(E_{ij}+E_{ji}). The off-diagonal entries of (X) vanish, and comparison
of the (ii) and (jj) positions makes all three diagonal entries equal.
Thus (X\in kI). \(\square\)

The exact replay writes these equations as a (27\times9) integer system of
rank eight, whose one-dimensional kernel is generated by (I_3). Lemma 2.1
applied to (S) and (R) proves the theorem.

## 3. Small exact checks

Three rational examples independently verify the block calculation:

- scalar (S,R) give cross rank six;
- a nonscalar diagonal (S) and scalar (R) give cross rank greater than six;
- a row shear (S) and scalar (R) also give cross rank greater than six.

As a counterexample screen only, the script streams all

\[
 3^9=19{,}683
\tag{3.1}
\]

matrices (S\in\operatorname{Mat}_3(\mathbf F_3)) in the more symmetric
subfamily (T=S\otimes I_2). Exactly 12,792 are invertible. Of these, exactly
two have cross rank at most six, namely the two nonzero scalar matrices.
This finite-field equality is not used in the characteristic-zero proof.

## 4. Boundary and next step

The remaining structural lemma is now precise: prove that an arbitrary invertible graph
(T:A_3\otimes P_2\to A_3\otimes Q_2) with cross rank at most six preserves
some (2+2) column matching. N6-117 proves rigidity after that matching is
known; it does not prove the matching exists.

The full (K_{2,3}/K_{3,2}) formal exhaustion, the six-color
(\kappa_2=0) endpoint, ordinary lower 29, exact unrestricted rank 32, and
border rank remain open.
