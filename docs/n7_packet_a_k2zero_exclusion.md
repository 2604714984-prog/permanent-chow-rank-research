# Exclusion of the Packet-A `K2=0` endpoint branch

## Status

`THE K2=0 BRANCH IS IMPOSSIBLE FOR A TRUE 49-TERM PERMANENT IDENTITY IN CHARACTERISTIC ZERO.`

This note closes only the extreme `K2=0` branch left by the Packet-A quotient
analysis.  It does not yet contradict the remaining branch with `K2 != 0` and
nonzero Hessian witnesses.

## 1. Endpoint dimensions force `H5=E5`

Let

\[
 A_2:M_2\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^2(V),
 \qquad
 A_5:M_5\longrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}^5(V),
\]

where

\[
 \dim M_2=\dim M_5=49\binom72=1029,
 \qquad K_d=\ker A_d.
\]

For a true 49-term identity at the rectangular endpoint, the preceding
kernel-image calculation gives

\[
 \dim K_2+\dim K_5=588.
\tag{1}
\]

Assume `K2=0`.  Then (1) gives

\[
 \dim K_5=588,
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}A_5=1029-588=441.
\tag{2}
\]

Write

\[
 H_5=\operatorname{im}A_5.
\]

Let `E5(perm_7)` be the degree-five derivative space of the permanent.  A
second derivative of a seven-factor product is a linear combination of its
21 labelled five-factor subproducts.  Differentiating the assumed identity
therefore gives

\[
 E_5(\operatorname{perm}_7)\subseteq H_5.
\tag{3}
\]

The nonzero degree-five derivatives of `perm_7` are the permanents of the
`5 x 5` submatrices obtained by retaining five rows and five columns.  There
are

\[
 \binom75^2=21^2=441
\]

such polynomials.  Distinct retained row/column pairs have disjoint monomial
supports, so they are linearly independent.  Hence

\[
 \dim E_5(\operatorname{perm}_7)=441.
\tag{4}
\]

Equations (2)--(4) prove

\[
 \boxed{H_5=E_5(\operatorname{perm}_7).}
\tag{5}
\]

In particular, every labelled product of five factors from every term is an
element of `E5(perm_7)`.

## 2. A UFD support lemma

Partition the 49 variables into the seven row groups

\[
 X_u=\{x_{u,0},\ldots,x_{u,6}\},
\]

and also into the seven column groups

\[
 Y_b=\{x_{0,b},\ldots,x_{6,b}\}.
\]

Every polynomial in `E5(perm_7)` has degree at most one in each row group and
at most one in each column group.  Indeed, each `5 x 5` permanent is
set-multilinear in both its retained rows and its retained columns, and this
property is preserved under linear combinations.

We use the following elementary lemma.

**Lemma.** Let `L_1,...,L_s` be nonzero linear forms in a polynomial ring over
a field.  If their product has degree at most one in a variable group `X`,
then at most one `L_j` has a nonzero `X`-component.

**Proof.** Regard the polynomial ring as a polynomial ring in `X` over the
domain generated by all other variables.  For nonzero polynomials,

\[
 \deg_X(fg)=\deg_X f+\deg_X g,
\]

because the product of their highest `X`-homogeneous components is nonzero.
A linear form has `X`-degree one exactly when its `X`-component is nonzero.
Thus

\[
 \deg_X\prod_jL_j
 =\#\{j:L_j\text{ has nonzero }X\text{-component}\}.
\]

If the left side is at most one, so is the count.  This argument also covers a
linear form that mixes variables from several different groups.  `QED`

Apply the lemma to a labelled five-factor product in (5), first with every row
group `X_u` and then with every column group `Y_b`.  Within that five-subset,
the row supports of its five factors are pairwise disjoint, and their column
supports are pairwise disjoint.

## 3. From all five-subsets to all seven factors

Fix one of the 49 terms and denote its seven nonzero factors by

\[
 \ell_1,\ldots,\ell_7.
\]

Every pair `ell_r,ell_s` occurs together in a five-subset: after choosing the
pair, choose any three of the remaining five factors.  The conclusion of the
support lemma for that five-subset therefore shows that

\[
 \operatorname{RowSupp}(\ell_r)
 \cap\operatorname{RowSupp}(\ell_s)=\varnothing,
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{ColSupp}(\ell_r)
 \cap\operatorname{ColSupp}(\ell_s)=\varnothing
\]

for every `r != s`.

Each nonzero linear factor has a nonempty row support.  Seven pairwise
disjoint nonempty subsets of a seven-element row set must all be singletons
and must partition the rows.  This rules out the possible boundary in which
one factor mixes several rows: such a factor would consume at least two row
groups and leave at most five groups for the other six nonzero factors.

The identical argument for columns shows that every factor has a singleton
column support and that the seven column supports partition the columns.
Consequently every factor is supported in the intersection of one row and one
column, hence is a scalar multiple of a single matrix coordinate.  After
reordering factors, each term has the form

\[
 \lambda_i\prod_{u=0}^6x_{u,\sigma_i(u)}
\tag{6}
\]

for some permutation `sigma_i in S_7` and nonzero scalar `lambda_i`.

## 4. Monomial-support contradiction

A sum of 49 terms of the form (6) is supported on at most 49 distinct
permutation monomials, even if some terms use the same permutation and their
coefficients combine or cancel.  In contrast,

\[
 \operatorname{perm}_7
 =\sum_{\sigma\in S_7}\prod_{u=0}^6x_{u,\sigma(u)}
\]

has all

\[
 7!=5040
\]

distinct permutation monomials with nonzero coefficient.  Monomials are
linearly independent, so the two polynomials cannot be equal.

This contradiction proves that no true characteristic-zero 49-term permanent
identity on the endpoint equality locus can have `K2=0`.

## 5. Remaining boundary

Combining this result with the exclusion of the all-Hessian-witness-zero
branch shows that any surviving Packet-A identity must satisfy

\[
 W^{\rm all}\ne0,
 \qquad K_2\ne0,
 \qquad K_5\ne0.
\]

The endpoint inclusion still requires

\[
 K_2^{\mathsf T}D^{-1}P K_5=0.
\]

The missing theorem is now narrower: prove that the permanent-specific
Hessian subspace of `K5` cannot be entirely orthogonal to the forced nonzero
`K2` under this inverse-coefficient pairing.  That incompatibility is not
proved here.  Packet A, the ordinary lower bound 50, and all border-rank
claims therefore remain open on the `K2 != 0` branch.
