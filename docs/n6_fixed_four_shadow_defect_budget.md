## 1. Exact shadow lower bounds for `20<=b<=27`

Use the notation of the fixed-four frontier:

\[
S=\mathcal D_3(\operatorname{perm}_6)
\cap
\mathcal D_3(R),
\qquad
b=\dim S.
\]

The projection argument gives

\[
\dim\partial S\le48.
\]

Bukh's two-dimensional shadow theorem says that, if

\[
b=\binom{x}{3}^2,
\]

then

\[
\dim\partial S\ge\binom{x}{2}^2.
\]

For each integer `b` from 20 through 27, choose the rational separator in the following table.

| `b` | separator `x_0` | `binom(x_0,3)^2<b` | `binom(x_0,2)^2>b+20` | conclusion |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | `41/10` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=41` |
| 21 | `103/25` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=42` |
| 22 | `207/50` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=43` |
| 23 | `104/25` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=44` |
| 24 | `209/50` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=45` |
| 25 | `21/5` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=46` |
| 26 | `211/50` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=47` |
| 27 | `106/25` | exact | exact | `dim partial S>=48` |

Monotonicity of the generalized binomial functions gives the uniform formula

\[
\boxed{
\dim\partial S\ge b+21
\qquad(20\le b\le27).
}
\tag{1.1}
\]

This is stronger than the single endpoint statement `b<=27`.

## 2. Defect variables for the four quadratic derivative spaces

For the four fixed terms, put

\[
G_i=\mathcal D_2(T_i),
\]

and define the nonnegative defects

\[
\varepsilon_i=15-\dim G_i,
\qquad
\alpha_i=3-\dim(E_2\cap G_i).
\tag{2.1}
\]

The projection proof of

\[
\dim(E_2\cap(G_1+\cdots+G_4))\le48
\]

can be applied after omitting any one factor. If the `j`th factor is omitted, it gives

\[
\dim\partial S
\le
\sum_{i\ne j}\dim G_i
+
\dim(E_2\cap G_j).
\]

Substituting (2.1) and the lower bound (1.1) yields:

### Proposition 2.1 — per-omitted-factor defect budget

For every `j=1,2,3,4`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i\ne j}\varepsilon_i+\alpha_j
\le
27-b.
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

Summing over all four choices gives

\[
\boxed{
3\sum_i\varepsilon_i+\sum_i\alpha_i
\le
4(27-b).
}
\tag{2.3}
\]

## 3. Endpoint consequences

### `b=27`

Equation (2.2) has zero right side. Therefore

\[
\varepsilon_i=0,
\qquad
\alpha_i=0
\]

for every `i`. All four terms satisfy

\[
\dim\mathcal D_2(T_i)=15,
\qquad
\dim(E_2\cap\mathcal D_2(T_i))=3,
\]

and

\[
\dim\partial S=48.
\]

Thus the top endpoint forces simultaneous equality in every individual quadratic-intersection bound and every projection bound.

### `b=26`

For every omitted factor,

\[
\sum_{i\ne j}\varepsilon_i+\alpha_j\le1.
\]

In particular, all but at most a tightly coupled unit defect must vanish. The total weighted defect obeys

\[
3\sum_i\varepsilon_i+\sum_i\alpha_i\le4.
\]

### General `b`

The right side `27-b` is at most seven. Hence every state in the 36-state frontier already lies near the equality geometry of four maximal quadratic derivative spaces. The structural problem is not an unconstrained four-term secant search.
