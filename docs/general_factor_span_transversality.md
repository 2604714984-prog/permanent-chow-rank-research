# Factor-span transversality for permanent derivative spaces

## Status and scope

`PROOF_DRAFT_COMPLETE`, `EXACT_INTEGER_ARITHMETIC_REPLAYED`,
`GENERAL_N_PROGRESS`.

This note proves a characteristic-zero transversality theorem between
permanent derivative spaces and low-dimensional symmetric powers. It then
combines the theorem with the existing multishadow and exact product-shadow
bounds to obtain

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_7)\ge 43,
\qquad
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_8)\ge 78.
}
\]

It also improves every reviewed general multishadow certificate from
`n=7` through `n=16` by a deterministic factor-span saving of one, two, or
three fixed terms.

This is an ordinary Chow-rank result. It does not prove the conjectural value
`2^(n-1)`, determine `perm_7` or `perm_8` exactly, establish a border-rank
improvement, or settle unrestricted `perm_6`. Literature novelty has not been
established.

## 1. Permanent derivative spaces

Let

\[
V_n=\operatorname{span}\{x_{ij}:0\le i,j<n\}
\]

and write

\[
E_d=\mathcal D_d(\operatorname{perm}_n).
\]

For row and column sets

\[
R,C\in\binom{[n]}d,
\]

let `p_(R,C)` be the corresponding `d x d` subpermanent. The vectors

\[
\{p_{R,C}:R,C\in\tbinom{[n]}d\}
\tag{1.1}
\]

form a basis of `E_d`. Under the row-column diagonal torus they have pairwise
distinct weights, indexed by the pair `(R,C)`.

## 2. The sub-square transversality theorem

### Theorem 2.1

Let

\[
L\subseteq V_n
\]

be a linear space. If

\[
\dim L<d^2,
\]

then

\[
\boxed{E_d\cap\operatorname{Sym}^dL=0.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The theorem holds over every characteristic-zero field.

### Proof

Extend scalars to an algebraic closure; dimensions are unchanged. Consider
the incidence variety

\[
\mathcal X=
\left\{
(L',[f])\in
\operatorname{Gr}(\ell,V_n)\times\mathbb P(E_d):
 f\in\operatorname{Sym}^dL'
\right\},
\qquad \ell=\dim L.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The condition in (2.2) is closed: over the Grassmannian,
`Sym^d(S)` is a vector subbundle of the trivial bundle with fiber
`Sym^d(V_n)`, where `S` is the tautological bundle. Hence `X` is projective.
It is also stable under the row-column diagonal torus.

If (2.1) failed, `X` would be nonempty. The closure of a torus orbit in a
projective variety contains a fixed point, so `X` would contain a fixed pair

\[
(L_0,[f_0]).
\]

A torus-fixed point of the Grassmannian is a coordinate space, say

\[
L_0=\operatorname{span}\{x_{ij}:(i,j)\in A\},
\qquad |A|=\ell.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Because the weights in (1.1) are multiplicity free, a torus-fixed line in
`E_d` is generated by one subpermanent:

\[
f_0=p_{R,C}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

If `p_(R,C)` belongs to `Sym^d(L_0)`, every variable appearing in one of its
monomials must belong to `A`. Every cell of the complete `d x d` rectangle
`R x C` lies in some perfect matching: after choosing a cell `(i,j)`, extend
that assignment to a bijection between the remaining `d-1` rows and columns.
Thus the union of the monomial supports of `p_(R,C)` is the full rectangle
`R x C`, containing `d^2` distinct variables. Consequently

\[
|A|\ge d^2,
\]

contradicting `ell<d^2`. Therefore `X` is empty and (2.1) follows. ∎

### Corollary 2.2 -- one Chow term

If

\[
T=\ell_1\cdots\ell_n
\]

is a degree-`n` Chow term and `n<d^2`, then

\[
\boxed{E_d\cap\mathcal D_d(T)=0.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

Indeed, with

\[
L_T=\operatorname{span}(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_n),
\]

one has `dim L_T<=n` and

\[
\mathcal D_d(T)\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^dL_T.
\]

No independence or nondegeneracy of the factors is required.

## 3. Several omitted Chow terms

The preceding argument gives more than one-term transversality.

### Corollary 3.1 -- an `s`-term block

Let `T_1,...,T_s` be degree-`n` Chow terms. If

\[
sn<d^2,
\tag{3.1}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
E_d\cap
\sum_{i=1}^s\mathcal D_d(T_i)=0.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Put

\[
L=\sum_{i=1}^sL_{T_i}.
\]

Then `dim L<=sn<d^2`, while every `D_d(T_i)` is contained in
`Sym^d(L)`. Apply Theorem 2.1. ∎

The largest universally admissible block size is

\[
\boxed{
s_{n,d}=\left\lfloor\frac{d^2-1}{n}\right\rfloor.
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

### Proposition 3.2 -- omitted-block projection bound

Let

\[
F_i=\mathcal D_d(T_i),
\qquad
U=F_1+\cdots+F_q,
\]

and choose any subset `I` of `s<=s_(n,d)` labels. Then

\[
\boxed{
\dim(E_d\cap U)
\le
\sum_{i\notin I}\dim F_i
\le
(q-s)\binom nd.
}
\tag{3.4}
\]

### Proof

Let

\[
\pi:\bigoplus_{i=1}^qF_i\longrightarrow U
\]

be the summation map. Choose a linear section of `pi` over the subspace
`E_d intersect U`. Project this section to the direct sum of the components
whose labels are outside `I`.

If an element lies in the kernel of that projection, its selected lift is
supported only on the labels in `I`. Its sum therefore lies in

\[
E_d\cap\sum_{i\in I}F_i,
\]

which is zero by Corollary 3.1. Hence the projection is injective and the
first inequality follows. A degree-`n` Chow term has

\[
\dim\mathcal D_d(T_i)\le\binom nd,
\]

including degenerate terms, proving the second inequality. ∎

The proof uses only literal derivative spaces as an ambient sum. It never
identifies that literal sum with the coupled catalectic image of
`T_1+...+T_q`.

## 4. Coupled application to multishadow removal

Choose an output degree `m` and put

\[
r=n-m,
\qquad
d=r-1.
\]

Let

\[
R=T_1+\cdots+T_q
\]

be a fixed part of a hypothetical decomposition and define the genuinely
coupled complementary intersection

\[
S=
\mathcal D_r(\operatorname{perm}_n)
\cap
\mathcal D_r(R),
\qquad b=\dim S.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Differentiation gives

\[
\partial S
\subseteq
E_d\cap\mathcal D_d(R).
\tag{4.2}
\]

The coupled image `D_d(R)` is contained in the literal sum of the individual
spaces. Therefore Proposition 3.2 gives, for every `s<=s_(n,d)`,

\[
\boxed{
\dim\partial S
\le(q-s)\binom nd.
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

This is the key saving. The previous general multishadow bound used the
weaker capacity `q*binom(n,d)`.

Let `F_(n,r)(b)` denote the exact product-shadow minimum from
`docs/general_exact_product_shadow.md`. Define

\[
\beta_{n,r}(q,s)=
\max\left\{
 b:F_{n,r}(b)\le(q-s)\binom nd
\right\}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Combining (4.4) with the already proved complementary-intersection residual
Koszul inequality gives:

### Theorem 4.1 -- factor-span exact multishadow bound

Assume the global lower bound already guarantees at least `q` terms and
`s<=s_(n,r-1)`. Whenever the residual numerator is positive,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_n)
\ge
q+
\left\lceil
\frac{
A_{n,m}-n^2\beta_{n,r}(q,s)
}{B_{n,m}}
\right\rceil,
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

where

\[
A_{n,m}
=n^2\binom nm^2-inom n{m+1}^2,
\qquad
B_{n,m}
=n^2\binom nm-inom n{m+1}.
\tag{4.6}
\]

## 5. Immediate reuse of every existing certificate

Suppose an existing multishadow certificate fixed `q_0` terms and used the
capacity

\[
q_0\binom nd.
\]

If the global lower bound guarantees `q_0+s_(n,d)` terms, fix that many terms
instead and omit an `s_(n,d)`-term block in Proposition 3.2. The new capacity
is unchanged:

\[
(q_0+s_{n,d}-s_{n,d})\binom nd
=q_0\binom nd.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Thus the old intersection cap and residual count remain valid, while the
number of fixed terms increases by `s_(n,d)`. Every such certificate improves
by exactly that amount.

For the reviewed central or lower-central certificates, the result is:

| `n` | shadow degree `d` | safe omitted terms | former bound | refined bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 3 | 1 | 41 | 42 |
| 8 | 3 | 1 | 76 | 77 |
| 9 | 4 | 1 | 141 | 142 |
| 10 | 4 | 1 | 267 | 268 |
| 11 | 5 | 2 | 506 | 508 |
| 12 | 5 | 2 | 968 | 970 |
| 13 | 6 | 2 | 1,853 | 1,855 |
| 14 | 6 | 2 | 3,568 | 3,570 |
| 15 | 7 | 3 | 6,879 | 6,882 |
| 16 | 7 | 3 | 13,312 | 13,315 |

In the central range the saving is

\[
s_{n,d}=\frac n4+O(1).
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is a genuine general-`n` improvement, although it is asymptotically much
smaller than the gap to Glynn's `2^(n-1)` upper bound.

## 6. Exact product-shadow applications

The exact product-shadow certificates for `n=7,8` permit one further unit of
improvement beyond the table in Section 5.

### 6.1 The seventh permanent

Take

\[
n=7,\quad m=3,\quad r=4,\quad d=3,\quad q=14,\quad s=1.
\]

The global first-Koszul lower bound is 36, so fixing fourteen terms is valid.
The derivative capacity is

\[
(q-s)\binom73=13\cdot35=455.
\]

The exact shadow transition is

\[
F_{7,4}(238)=452,
\qquad
F_{7,4}(239)=456.
\]

Hence `b<=238`. The residual requires

\[
\left\lceil
\frac{58,800-49\cdot238}{1,680}
\right\rceil
=29
\]

terms. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_7)
\ge14+29=43.
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Glynn gives the upper bound 64.

### 6.2 The eighth permanent

Take

\[
n=8,\quad m=r=4,\quad d=3,\quad q=15,\quad s=1.
\]

The global first-Koszul lower bound is 71. The derivative capacity is

\[
(q-s)\binom83=14\cdot56=784.
\]

The exact shadow transition is

\[
F_{8,4}(560)=784,
\qquad
F_{8,4}(561)=793.
\]

Hence `b<=560`. The residual requires

\[
\left\lceil
\frac{310,464-64\cdot560}{4,424}
\right\rceil
=63
\]

terms. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_8)
\ge15+63=78.
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

Glynn gives the upper bound 128.

## 7. Why this does not solve the general conjecture

The theorem recovers information discarded by a dimension-only derivative
capacity: a small block of Chow terms has a combined factor span too small to
contain any permanent derivative. In the central range it saves about `n/4`
terms.

That is structurally useful but asymptotically insufficient. The current lower
bounds remain on the central-binomial or multishadow scale, while Glynn's
upper bound is `2^(n-1)`. Closing the conjecture still requires an invariant
that retains more of the coupled relation or equality-locus geometry.

The strongest objection is therefore valid: factor-span transversality can
improve every certificate while leaving the main asymptotic barrier intact.
The result should be retained as a reusable lemma and not expanded into a new
state-management framework.
