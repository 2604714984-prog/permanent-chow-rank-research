# The fifty-dimensional product-shadow equality locus

**Status.** `PURE_CHARACTERISTIC_ZERO_EQUALITY_LOCUS_CLASSIFICATION`,
`EXACT_QQ_QUADRATIC_ELIMINATION`, `EXACT_SYMBOLIC_240_BRANCH_REPLAY`
(N6-064).  The base field is algebraically closed of characteristic zero.
This theorem classifies equality in the permanent product-shadow inequality;
it does not by itself exclude the remaining Chow endpoint at \(b=50\).

Put

\[
 E_3=\langle p_{R,C}:R,C\in\tbinom{[6]}3\rangle,
 \qquad
 E_2=\langle p_{I,J}:I,J\in\tbinom{[6]}2\rangle.
\]

N6-056 proves that every fifty-plane \(S\subset E_3\) satisfies

\[
 \dim\partial S\geq75.
\tag{1.1}
\]

N6-062A classifies the coordinate equality planes.  Up to independent row
and column permutations they are the two orientations of

\[
 S_0=
 \left(\binom{U_4}3\otimes\binom{V_5}3\right)
 +\left(\binom{U_3}3\otimes\binom{[6]}3\right),
 \qquad U_3\subset U_4.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Its derivative image is

\[
 K_0=
 \left(\binom{U_4}2\otimes\binom{V_5}2\right)
 +\left(\binom{U_3}2\otimes\binom{[6]}2\right),
 \qquad \dim K_0=75.
\tag{1.3}
\]

## 1. Boolean replacement branches

On squarefree \(d\)-subsets define

\[
 N^{(d)}_{a\to z}e_R=
 \begin{cases}
 e_{R-\{a\}+\{z\}},&a\in R,\ z\notin R,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\tag{1.4}
\]

Choose

\[
 \rho_3\in U_3,\quad \rho_4,\rho_5\in U_4,
 \quad \kappa_5\in V_5.
\]

Using four independent parameters, compose the row replacements

\[
 (I+t_5N_{\rho_5\to5})(I+t_4N_{\rho_4\to4})
 (I+t_3N_{\rho_3\to3})
\tag{1.5}
\]

in degree three and degree two, and compose the column replacement
\(I+sN_{\kappa_5\to5}\).  Apply the degree-three maps to \(S_0\) and the
degree-two maps to \(K_0\).

There are

\[
 3\cdot4\cdot4\cdot5=240
\tag{1.6}
\]

such four-parameter families.  Exact polynomial differentiation, followed by
the inverse degree-two replacements in reverse order, gives

\[
 \partial S_{\boldsymbol t}\subset K_{\boldsymbol t}
 \quad\text{identically in }\mathbb Z[t_3,t_4,t_5,s].
\tag{1.7}
\]

Since the transformations are invertible and \(\dim K_0=75\), while (1.1)
is a lower bound, equality holds.  Four selected Grassmann-chart coordinates
have identity Jacobian in the four parameters.  Their linear coefficients
are exactly the corresponding Grassmann graph coordinates, rather than
coordinates in an auxiliary parametrization.  Thus every family is a closed
smooth formal four-fold at its coordinate endpoint.  The same exact symbolic
calculation in degrees two and one verifies

\[
 \partial K_{\boldsymbol t}\subset M_{\boldsymbol t}
 \quad\text{identically in }\mathbb Z[t_3,t_4,t_5,s].
\tag{1.8}
\]

## 2. The local incidence equations

Let \(X\subset\operatorname{Gr}(50,E_3)\) be the rank-at-most-75 locus.
Near \(S_0\), introduce the incidence of a 75-plane \(K\subset E_2\) with
\(\partial S\subset K\).  By (1.1), every point of \(X\) has derivative
rank exactly 75, so \(K=\partial S\) is unique and this incidence projects
isomorphically to \(X\) locally.

In graph coordinates

\[
 t:S_0\to E_3/S_0,
 \qquad \eta:K_0\to E_2/K_0,
\]

the equations are

\[
 qD(t)-\eta P_0-\eta pD(t)=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

The linear part is \(qD(t)-\eta P_0\).  Each coefficient is one equality
between a \(t\)-variable and an \(\eta\)-variable, one grounded variable, or
zero.  Exact connected-component elimination leaves sixteen free variables,
in four torus-weight groups of sizes

\[
 3,\quad5,\quad4,\quad4.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Use the formal implicit-function theorem to eliminate all transverse
variables and write the completed local ring as

\[
 k[[x_1,\ldots,x_{16}]]/I.
\]

Let

\[
 J=I(K_3)+I(K_5)+I(K_4)+I(K_4),
\tag{2.3}
\]

where \(I(K_m)\) is generated by \(x_ix_j\), \(i<j\), inside one group.
Thus \(J\) has 25 squarefree quadratic generators and is the intersection of
240 coordinate facet primes, each selecting one variable from every group.

Among (2.1) are 1,140 coefficient equations whose complete linear part is
zero.  In such an equation, the order-two contribution after formal
elimination is obtained by substituting the linear free-variable solution
into \(-\eta pD(t)\): transverse variables begin in order two and contribute
only from order three onward.  In the 136 quadratic monomials, the exact
rational matrix has size

\[
 1140\times136,
\]

rank 25, and reduced row space exactly the 25 unit vectors generating (2.3).
Therefore

\[
 J\subset\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}(I).
\tag{2.4}
\]

## 3. Tangent-cone sandwich and formal exhaustion

For each facet prime \(P\) of \(J\), Section 1 supplies a closed smooth
formal branch \(B_P\subset X\) whose tangent cone is exactly that facet.
Ideals reverse under inclusion, so

\[
 I(X)\subset I(B_P)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}I(X)\subset P.
\]

Intersecting all facets and using (2.4) gives

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{in}_{\mathfrak m}(I)=J.}
\tag{3.1}
\]

This uses the full quadratic certificate and all exact branches, not tangent
dimension alone.

Let \(B=\bigcup_PB_P\) and \(I_B=\bigcap_PI(B_P)\).  We only use the valid
inclusion

\[
 \operatorname{in}(I_B)\subset\bigcap_P\operatorname{in}I(B_P)=J;
\]

we do not assert that initial ideals commute with intersections.  Since
\(I\subset I_B\), (3.1) yields

\[
 J=\operatorname{in}I\subset\operatorname{in}I_B\subset J.
\]

Two nested closed ideals in a Noetherian complete local ring with the same
\(\mathfrak m\)-adic initial ideal are equal.  Indeed, repeatedly subtract
from an element of the larger ideal an element of the smaller ideal with the
same lowest homogeneous form.  The remainders tend to zero, while the
subtracted series converges inside the closed smaller ideal.  Hence

\[
 I=I_B.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the complete local equality scheme is precisely the union of the 240
Boolean-replacement branches.  The transposed coordinate hook has the same
classification after exchanging the two factors.

## 4. Global classification

The equality locus \(X\) is projective and stable under the connected
row-column torus.  Every irreducible component is torus stable and contains a
torus-fixed point.  N6-062A says that each such point is a coordinate hook or
its transpose.  The local classification (3.2) then places the component on
one Boolean-replacement branch.  Therefore every irreducible component of
the equality locus is the closure of one of the families in Section 1 or its
transpose.

## 5. The second shadow

The same replacement maps give

\[
 K_{\boldsymbol t}=
 G_2\binom{U_4}2\otimes H_2\binom{V_5}2
 +G_2\binom{U_3}2\otimes\binom{[6]}2.
\]

Its derivative lies in the 23-dimensional hook

\[
 M_{\boldsymbol t}=
 G_1U_4\otimes H_1V_5+G_1U_3\otimes k^6.
\tag{5.1}
\]

The exact pair-to-singleton product-shadow minimum for a 75-plane is 23, so
the containment is equality:

\[
 \boxed{\dim\partial K_{\boldsymbol t}=23.}
\tag{5.2}
\]

Consequently every finite-parameter point on a displayed branch has a
23-dimensional second shadow of the form (5.1).  On the projective closure,
second-shadow rank is at most 23 by specialization and at least 23 by the
universal lower bound, so it remains exactly 23.

There is a slightly stronger intrinsic description at the boundary.  The
projective parameter space

\[
 \operatorname{Fl}(3,4;k^6)\times\operatorname{Gr}(5,k^6)
\]

maps regularly to \(\operatorname{Gr}(23,k^6\otimes k^6)\) by

\[
 (R_3\subset R_4,C_5)\longmapsto
 R_4\otimes C_5+R_3\otimes k^6.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Indeed, the intersection is the constant-rank space \(R_3\otimes C_5\), so
the displayed sum is a rank-23 subbundle image.  The parameter space is
projective, hence its image is closed.  Every finite Boolean branch has the
form (5.3) in degree one, and therefore every boundary second shadow is still
a genuine flag hook (5.3).  It may leave the chosen finite Boolean-shear
chart, but it does not leave the projective flag-hook image.

## 6. Boundary and replay

At the unresolved all-`alpha=3`, \(b=50\) endpoint, write \(F_i\) for the
six literal-direct 15-dimensional quadratic Chow spaces and \(W\) for their
common 15-dimensional quotient.  Choose the induced sections
\(s_i:W\to F_i\).  With one index fixed, the five difference graphs

\[
 D_{i1}=\{s_i(w)-s_1(w):w\in W\}\qquad(2\le i\le6)
\]

span \(K=E_2\cap H_2\).  Each has dimension 15 and satisfies

\[
 \partial D_{i1}\subset L_i+L_1,
 \qquad \dim L_i,\dim L_1\le6.
\]

The universal product-shadow bound for a 15-plane of permanent quadrics is
12.  Hence every displayed containment is equality throughout: the two
factor spans are six-dimensional and transverse, and

\[
 \partial D_{i1}=L_i\oplus L_1.
\]

It follows that

\[
 \partial K=\sum_{i=1}^6L_i=:M.
\]

Since \(\partial S=K\), the present theorem proves that this common
23-plane \(M\) is a genuine flag hook of the form (5.3), although it need not
belong to the chosen finite Boolean-shear chart.  G-048 shows that containment
in a 23-plane of the hook type, even
together with pairwise transversality, does not by itself force an N6-061
transverse pair.  Excluding the Chow endpoint still requires using the
section-difference spaces, their common quotient and cocycle, or Chow-frame
realizability in a degeneration-stable way.
