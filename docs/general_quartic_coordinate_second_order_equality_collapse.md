# Coordinate second-order equality-state collapse

Status: `ROUTE_DIAGNOSTIC_AND_EQUALITY_STATE_LEMMA`, characteristic zero.

This note does **not** prove a six-block zero theorem. It identifies the sharp
states of a safe second-order coordinate over-envelope and proves that their
apparent internal-kernel advantage disappears once first-order integrability
is imposed on a single component.

## 1. Safe second-order over-envelope

For an unordered multiset `gamma` of six coordinate cells in one `4 x 4`
block, let

\[
E_2(\gamma)=\{M:\ |M\cap A_\gamma|\ge2\}.
\]

Let `D(gamma)` be the perfect matchings already contained in the frame. Group
the fifteen four-label source subsets by their coordinate monomial. For each
source fiber, allow one or two labeled factors to move. If the target-matching
indicator is nonconstant on the fiber, record that matching in
`K2_tilde(gamma)`. Put

\[
\widetilde S_2(\gamma)=D(\gamma)\cup\widetilde K_2(\gamma).
\]

This deliberately ignores the requirement that the first-order coefficient
vanish, so it is an upper envelope for internally generated second-order
matching support.

Exact enumeration of all

\[
\binom{16+6-1}{6}=54,264
\]

coordinate multisets gives

\[
\boxed{|E_2(\gamma)|+|\widetilde S_2(\gamma)|\le20}.
\]

There are exactly 288 equality frames. Their profile is

```text
|E2| = 12
|D| = 0
|K2_tilde| = 8
|S2_tilde| = 8
```

and they form two row-column orbits of size 144, exchanged by transpose. Orbit
representatives are

```text
(0,0,1,2,7,11)
(0,0,4,8,13,14)
```

Every equality frame has five distinct cells, multiplicity pattern
`(2,1,1,1,1)`, and no perfect matching retaining three frame cells.

## 2. Exact integrability collapse on an equality frame

Write an equality frame as

\[
(x,x,a,b,c,d),
\]

where the five coordinate variables are distinct. The specialization kernel of
the squarefree four-label source is generated by swapping the two labels over
`x`. Hence every order-zero kernel vector has polynomial form

\[
(\ell_0-\ell_1)H,
\]

where

\[
H\in\operatorname{span}\{bcd,acd,abd,abc\}.
\]

Let

\[
\delta=\ell_0'(0)-\ell_1'(0).
\]

If a single component satisfies `g_0=0`, then its order-one coefficient from
this kernel vector is `delta H`. To also have `g_1=0`, source motion must cancel
this term inside the coordinate source image

\[
W=\operatorname{span}\{x^2uv,\ xuvw,\ abcd\},
\]

with the evident choices of distinct variables among `a,b,c,d`.

Monomial comparison gives the exact condition:

- if `H` has at least two nonzero triple-product coefficients, then
  `delta` is a scalar multiple of `x`;
- if `H` is one triple product, say `bcd`, then `delta` lies in
  `span{x,a}`, where `a` is the omitted variable.

Thus every coordinate direction occurring in `delta H` is already a frame
cell.

At order two there are four sources of terms:

1. direct order-two source motion;
2. first factor motion applied to order-one source motion;
3. second factor motion applied to the order-zero kernel vector; and
4. the product of `delta` with the first variation of `H`.

Any perfect-matching monomial in cases 2--4 retains at least three distinct
frame cells. Case 1 would require a perfect matching already contained in the
frame. But every equality frame has

\[
E_1(\gamma)=\{M:|M\cap A_\gamma|\ge3\}=\varnothing
\]

and `D(gamma)=empty`. Therefore:

### Theorem

For every one of the 288 second-order equality frames, if a single component
has both its order-zero and order-one coefficients equal to zero, then

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{proj}_{M_4} g_2=0.}
\]

Consequently the eight apparent internal-kernel matchings in the safe
over-envelope are not individually integrable two-jets. Any global
six-component second-order survivor using these equality frames must rely on
cross-component cancellation at order zero or one.
