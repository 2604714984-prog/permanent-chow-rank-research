## 1. Statement

Let `A` and `B` be six-dimensional vector spaces with coordinate bases

\[
a_0,\ldots,a_5,
\qquad
b_0,\ldots,b_5,
\]

and identify

\[
V=A\otimes B
\]

with the matrix-variable space by

\[
x_{ij}=a_i\otimes b_j.
\]

Put

\[
E_2=\mathcal D_2(\operatorname{perm}_6)
\subseteq
\operatorname{Sym}^2V.
\]

Thus `E_2` is spanned by the 225 rectangle quadrics

\[
q_{ii';jj'}
=
x_{ij}x_{i'j'}+x_{ij'}x_{i'j}.
\]

For a linear subspace `L subset V`, write

\[
Q_L=E_2\cap\operatorname{Sym}^2L.
\]

The universal single-term proof already established

\[
\dim Q_L\le3
\qquad
(\dim L\le6).
\tag{1.1}
\]

This note classifies every equality case.

### Theorem 1.1 — extremal six-plane classification

Work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let

\[
L\subseteq A\otimes B,
\qquad
\dim L\le6.
\]

Then

\[
\dim Q_L=3
\]

if and only if `dim L=6` and, after possibly transposing `A` and `B`, there are set partitions

\[
[6]=R_1\sqcup R_2,
\qquad
[6]=C_1\sqcup C_2\sqcup C_3,
\tag{1.2}
\]

and nonzero vectors

\[
u_s\in\operatorname{span}\{a_i:i\in R_s\},
\qquad
v_t\in\operatorname{span}\{b_j:j\in C_t\},
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
L
=
\operatorname{span}(u_1,u_2)
\otimes
\operatorname{span}(v_1,v_2,v_3).
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The supports in each family are pairwise disjoint. In this case

\[
\boxed{
Q_L
=
\operatorname{span}
\left\{
(u_1u_2)\otimes(v_rv_s):1\le r<s\le3
\right\},
}
\tag{1.4}
\]

where the Cauchy embedding sends

\[
(u_1u_2)\otimes(v_rv_s)
\]

to

\[
(u_1\otimes v_r)(u_2\otimes v_s)
+
(u_1\otimes v_s)(u_2\otimes v_r).
\]

There are

\[
S(6,2)S(6,3)=31\cdot90=2790
\]

irreducible support components of row-`2` / column-`3` type and the same number of transposed components. Hence the reduced extremal locus has

\[
\boxed{5580}
\]

irreducible components, each of dimension seven.

For a non-algebraically-closed characteristic-zero field, the same statement holds after scalar extension to its algebraic closure. The classified support components themselves are defined over the prime field.

## 2. Tensor description of `E_2`

Let

\[
A^\circ
=
\operatorname{span}\{a_ia_{i'}:i<i'\}
\subseteq
\operatorname{Sym}^2A,
\]

and define `B^circ` similarly. The Cauchy decomposition gives an injective map

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2A\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2B
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Sym}^2(A\otimes B)
\]

whose restriction identifies

\[
\boxed{
E_2=A^\circ\otimes B^\circ.
}
\tag{2.1}
\]

The zero-diagonal condition is the key geometry. A nonzero rank-two element of `A^circ` has the form

\[
uv
\]

with `u` and `v` supported on disjoint coordinate sets. Conversely, disjoint support makes `uv` zero-diagonal. The same statement holds in `B`.

If `L` has the form (1.3), then

\[
A^\circ\cap\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{span}(u_1,u_2)
=
\operatorname{span}(u_1u_2),
\]

while

\[
B^\circ\cap\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{span}(v_1,v_2,v_3)
=
\operatorname{span}\{v_1v_2,v_1v_3,v_2v_3\}.
\]

Equation (1.4) follows, proving the easy direction of Theorem 1.1.

## 3. The closed extremal locus and its fixed points

Let

\[
\mathcal X
=
\left\{
L\in\operatorname{Gr}(6,V):
\dim(E_2\cap\operatorname{Sym}^2L)\ge3
\right\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

It is a closed determinantal subvariety. Indeed, for each `L` consider

\[
\mu_L:
\operatorname{Sym}^2L
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Sym}^2V/E_2.
\]

Since `dim Sym^2 L=21`, condition (3.1) is exactly

\[
\operatorname{rank}\mu_L\le18.
\tag{3.2}
\]

The row-column torus acts on `X`. Its fixed six-planes are coordinate six-planes, equivalently edge sets of simple bipartite graphs with six edges.

For a coordinate plane, `Q_L` is the rectangle space of that graph. A six-edge bipartite graph with at least three rectangles is exactly

\[
K_{2,3}
\quad\text{or}\quad
K_{3,2},
\]

and has precisely three rectangles. Therefore the torus fixed points of `X` are exactly the coordinate `K_2,3` and `K_3,2` planes.

It is enough to analyze one representative,

\[
L_0
=
\operatorname{span}
\{x_{ij}:i\in\{0,1\},\ j\in\{0,1,2\}\}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Transposition covers the other orientation.

## 4. Exact local rank chart at `L_0`

The Grassmann chart at `L_0` has

\[
6(36-6)=180
\]

coordinates. Write a nearby plane as the graph of

\[
\phi:L_0\longrightarrow V/L_0.
\]

The matrix of `mu_L` has 21 columns and 441 quotient rows. At `L_0` it has rank 18 and kernel dimension three. The audit deterministically selects an `18 x 18` pivot minor with

\[
\boxed{\det=1.}
\tag{4.1}
\]

The remaining three columns are eliminated by this unimodular block. The resulting Schur residual gives local defining equations for `X`.

### Proposition 4.1 — exact tangent space

The Jacobian of the local residual has characteristic-zero rank

\[
\boxed{163}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Its kernel has dimension 17 and consists exactly of the following tensor-product directions:

- eight row-plane directions
  \[
  \alpha_{sr},
  \qquad
  s\in\{0,1\},
  \quad
  r\in\{2,3,4,5\};
  \]
- nine column-plane directions
  \[
  \beta_{ct},
  \qquad
  c\in\{0,1,2\},
  \quad
  t\in\{3,4,5\}.
  \]

Here `alpha_sr` simultaneously sends

\[
x_{s0},x_{s1},x_{s2}
\]

to

\[
x_{r0},x_{r1},x_{r2},
\]

and `beta_ct` simultaneously sends

\[
x_{0c},x_{1c}
\]

to

\[
x_{0t},x_{1t}.
\]

### Exact characteristic-zero justification

The script reconstructs all 180 integer Jacobian columns. Their rank modulo

\[

\[
180-17=163.
\]

Thus (4.2) is exact over characteristic zero; no finite-field equality is extrapolated without a matching upper bound.

## 5. The thirteen second-order obstructions

After the 163 transverse linear coordinates are eliminated, use the 17 tangent coordinates from Proposition 4.1. The exact second-order obstruction quotient has rank 13. Its generators are the squarefree monomials

\[
\boxed{
\alpha_{0r}\alpha_{1r}
\qquad
(r=2,3,4,5),
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\beta_{0t}\beta_{1t},
\quad
\beta_{0t}\beta_{2t},
\quad
\beta_{1t}\beta_{2t}
\qquad
(t=3,4,5).
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

These are exactly the violations of coordinatewise disjoint support.

### Proposition 5.1 — exact obstruction certificate

The thirteen classes in (5.1)–(5.2) are linearly independent modulo the Jacobian image over characteristic zero. Every other quadratic monomial in the 17 tangent variables has an explicit integral second-order correction.

### Proof

There are

\[
\binom{17+1}{2}=153
\]

quadratic monomials. The audit treats all of them.

1. Every square has zero second-order residual.
2. Compatible row-row and column-column pairs have zero second-order residual.
3. For a row-column pair
   \[
   \alpha_{sr}\beta_{ct},
   \]
   the integral correction is the graph coordinate sending
   \[
   x_{sc}\longmapsto x_{rt}.
   \]
   This is the quadratic term in
   \[
   (a_s+\alpha a_r)\otimes(b_c+\beta b_t).
   \]
4. The remaining thirteen residual classes have rank 13 modulo `1,000,003` after quotienting by the 163-dimensional Jacobian image.

Items 1–3 are exact integer identities, so the obstruction rank over characteristic zero is at most 13. Item 4 gives the matching characteristic-zero lower bound. ∎

Therefore the degree-two initial ideal of the completed local equations contains

\[
I=
(\alpha_{0r}\alpha_{1r}:2\le r\le5)
+
(\beta_{0t}\beta_{1t},
 \beta_{0t}\beta_{2t},
 \beta_{1t}\beta_{2t}:3\le t\le5).
\tag{5.3}
\]

## 6. The local branch count and multiplicity closure

The variables in (5.3) split into disjoint groups.

- For every outside row `r`, the factor ideal is
  \[
  (\alpha_{0r}\alpha_{1r}),
  \]
  with two minimal primes.
- For every outside column `t`, the factor ideal is the edge ideal of a triangle,
  \[
  (\beta_{0t}\beta_{1t},
   \beta_{0t}\beta_{2t},
   \beta_{1t}\beta_{2t}),
  \]
  with three minimal primes.

Hence `I` is squarefree, equidimensional of dimension

\[
4+3=7,
\]

and has

\[
\boxed{
2^4\,3^3=432
}
\tag{6.1}
\]

minimal coordinate primes and multiplicity 432.

### Explicit branches

Choose functions

\[
\rho:\{2,3,4,5\}\longrightarrow\{0,1\},
\]

\[
\kappa:\{3,4,5\}\longrightarrow\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Define

\[
u_s
=a_s+
\sum_{\rho(r)=s}\alpha_r a_r,
\]

and

\[
v_c
=b_c+
\sum_{\kappa(t)=c}\beta_t b_t.
\]

Then

\[
L_{\rho,\kappa}
=
\operatorname{span}(u_0,u_1)
\otimes
\operatorname{span}(v_0,v_1,v_2)
\tag{6.2}
\]

is a seven-parameter smooth family through `L_0`. The row vectors have disjoint supports, as do the column vectors, so (1.4) gives

\[
\dim Q_{L_{\rho,\kappa}}=3.
\]

There are exactly 432 pairs `(rho,kappa)`, and their tangent spaces are the 432 distinct coordinate seven-planes that are the minimal primes of `I`.

### Proposition 6.1 — no additional local components

The reduced germ of `X` at `L_0` is exactly the union of the 432 branches (6.2).

### Proof

After eliminating the 163 independent linear equations, the completed local ring has 17 parameters. Since its initial ideal contains `I`, its dimension is at most seven and its Hilbert--Samuel multiplicity is at most 432.

Each family (6.2) is an irreducible seven-dimensional subvariety contained in `X`. Therefore each is a local irreducible component. Their tangent primes are distinct, so there are at least 432 local components, each contributing multiplicity at least one. The local multiplicity is consequently at least 432.

Both inequalities are equalities. Thus there is no room for another component or for additional multiplicity. The reduced local germ is precisely the displayed union. ∎

This multiplicity comparison is the step that upgrades a second-order calculation into a complete local component classification. Merely listing the tangent directions would not suffice.

## 7. Globalization by the row-column torus

The variety `X` is projective and invariant under the connected row-column torus. A connected group cannot nontrivially permute the finite set of irreducible components, so every irreducible component of `X` is torus-stable. By the Borel fixed-point theorem, each component contains a torus fixed point.

Section 3 classified those fixed points as coordinate `K_2,3` and `K_3,2` planes. Proposition 6.1 and transposition classify every reduced component through every such point. Therefore every irreducible component of `X` is one of the disjoint-support tensor-product components in Theorem 1.1.

Conversely, Section 2 shows that every such component lies in `X`. This proves the theorem.

## 8. Global component count

A maximal row-`2` / column-`3` component is determined by:

1. an unordered partition of the six row coordinates into two nonempty blocks;
2. an unordered partition of the six column coordinates into three nonempty blocks.

The counts are the Stirling numbers

\[
S(6,2)=31,
\qquad
S(6,3)=90.
\]

For a fixed pair of partitions, the component is the image of

\[
\prod_{s=1}^2\mathbb P(k^{R_s})
\times
\prod_{t=1}^3\mathbb P(k^{C_t}),
\]

and has dimension

\[
(6-2)+(6-3)=7.
\]

Thus there are 2790 components of this orientation. Transposition supplies another 2790, giving 5580 in total.

## 10. The factor-frame base locus

The six-plane classification does not yet classify the six Chow factors inside an extremal plane. It reduces that problem to a small projective frame locus.

Use the row-`2` / column-`3` coordinates

\[
y_{sc}=u_s\otimes v_c,
\qquad
s\in\{0,1\},
\quad
c\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Let a covector in `L^*` have coordinates

\[
z=(a_0,a_1,a_2;b_0,b_1,b_2).
\]

The common zero locus of the three quadrics in `Q_L` is

\[
\mathcal Z_Q
=
\left\{
[z]\in\mathbb P^5:
 a_ib_j+a_jb_i=0
 \text{ for all }i<j
\right\}.
\tag{10.1}
\]

### Proposition 10.1 — five-component base locus

Set-theoretically,

\[
\mathcal Z_Q
=
\mathbb P\{b=0\}
\cup
\mathbb P\{a=0\}
\cup
\bigcup_{0\le i<j\le2}\mathcal Q_{ij},
\tag{10.2}
\]

where, for `{i,j,k}={0,1,2}`,

\[
\mathcal Q_{ij}
=
\left\{
 a_k=b_k=0,
 \quad
 a_ib_j+a_jb_i=0
\right\}
\subseteq\mathbb P^3
\]

is a smooth quadric surface.

### Proof

Associate to every column the vector

\[
w_i=(a_i,b_i)\in k^2
\]

and use the nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form

\[
\langle(a,b),(c,d)\rangle=ad+bc.
\]

Equation (10.1) says that the three `w_i` are pairwise orthogonal. If all nonzero `w_i` lie on the same isotropic line, then either `a=0` or `b=0`. Otherwise a two-dimensional nondegenerate space cannot contain three nonzero pairwise orthogonal vectors, so at most two columns are nonzero. This gives one of the three quadrics `Q_ij`. The reverse inclusion is immediate. ∎

Let `z_1,...,z_6` be the basis of `L^*` dual to the Chow factors `ell_1,...,ell_6`. A quadratic form belongs to

\[
F_2=\operatorname{span}\{\ell_i\ell_j:i<j\}
\]

exactly when all six diagonal coefficients in the factor basis vanish. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
Q_L\subseteq F_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
[z_1],\ldots,[z_6]\in\mathcal Z_Q.
}
\tag{10.3}
\]

Thus an extremal Chow term is equivalently a projective frame of six points on the union of two planes and three quadric surfaces in (10.2).
