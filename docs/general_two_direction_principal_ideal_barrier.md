# Principal two-direction ideal profiles stop at the central binomial bound

## Status and claim boundary

`PROOF_DRAFT_COMPLETE`, `GENERAL_N_ROUTE_BARRIER`,
`EXACT_FINITE_INTERFACE_REPLAYED`.

This note continues the two-direction apolar-module program after the homogeneous
power-profile diagnostic. It treats every **principal homogeneous ideal** in a
binary differential algebra, not merely a power of one selected linear form.

The conclusion is

\[
\boxed{
\text{every principal two-direction ideal profile for }\operatorname{perm}_n
\text{ is bounded by }
\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
}
\]

Thus an ideal-profile lower bound that exceeds the central binomial coefficient
must use at least two genuinely active minimal generators at the tested output
degree.

This is a ceiling for a named monotone invariant. It is not an upper bound on
the actual Chow rank, does not improve any finite-`n` lower bound, and does not
close nonprincipal ideals, multigraded relation modules, representation-valued
invariants, valuative arguments, or Chow-realizability defects.

## 1. Apolar subquotient input

Let

\[
A_f=S/f^\perp
\]

be the graded apolar algebra. Fix a differential subspace

\[
W\subseteq S_1,
\qquad
\dim W\le2.
\]

If

\[
f=T_1+\cdots+T_r,
\]

then

\[
\bigcap_iT_i^\perp\subseteq f^\perp.
\]

Consequently

\[
S/\bigcap_iT_i^\perp
\hookrightarrow
\bigoplus_iA_{T_i}
\]

and the same intermediate quotient surjects onto `A_f`. Therefore `A_f` is a
`k[W]`-subquotient of the direct sum of the termwise apolar algebras.

The intermediate quotient is essential. No equality between the coupled
apolar object of the sum and a literal direct sum of termwise objects is used.

## 2. Principal profiles

Let

\[
0\ne g\in\operatorname{Sym}^p(W),
\qquad
1\le p\le d\le n.
\]

For a graded `k[W]`-module `M`, define

\[
\lambda_{g,d}(M)
=
\dim\bigl(gM_{d-p}\bigr).
\tag{2.1}
\]

This is additive on direct sums. If `N` is a submodule or quotient of `M`, the
image of multiplication by `g` on `N` is a subquotient of the corresponding
image on `M`; hence

\[
\lambda_{g,d}(N)\le\lambda_{g,d}(M).
\tag{2.2}
\]

Thus (2.1) is a valid lower-bound invariant under the apolar subquotient
relation.

## 3. Exact one-term Boolean envelope

For a degree-`n` Chow term, the preceding two-direction theorem gives a
squarefree Boolean envelope

\[
B_n
=
k[z_1,\ldots,z_n]/(z_1^2,\ldots,z_n^2).
\]

Write

\[
h_j=\dim(B_n)_j=\binom nj.
\]

Define the exact principal one-term envelope

\[
\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}
=
\max_{\substack{U\subseteq(B_n)_1,\ \dim U\le2\\
0\ne g\in\operatorname{Sym}^p(U)}}
\operatorname{rank}
\left(
 g:(B_n)_{d-p}\longrightarrow(B_n)_d
\right).
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

\[
\boxed{
\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}
=
\min\left\{
\binom n{d-p},
\binom nd
\right\}.
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Every linear map in (3.1) has rank at most the smaller of its source and target
dimensions, which gives the upper bound in (3.2).

For the reverse inequality choose the one-dimensional subspace generated by

\[
L=z_1+\cdots+z_n
\]

and take

\[
g=L^p.
\]

The Boolean complete intersection is strong Lefschetz in characteristic zero.
Equivalently, multiplication by `L^p` between degrees `d-p` and `d` has maximal
rank. A self-contained formulation uses the standard `sl_2` operators

\[
E(m)=Lm,
\qquad
F(z_I)=\sum_{i\in I}z_{I\setminus\{i\}},
\]

whose commutator acts on degree `j` by the scalar `n-2j`. The resulting
`sl_2` decomposition gives maximal rank for every power of `E`. Hence the
source/target upper bound is attained. ∎

The maximization in (3.1) allows a one-dimensional image even though the global
research interface permits two directions. Therefore the strong-Lefschetz
witness is legal.

## 4. Permanent numerator and the route ceiling

The permanent apolar algebra has Hilbert function

\[
\dim(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})_j
=
\binom nj^2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For any two-plane `W` and any nonzero homogeneous `g` of degree `p`, the
multiplication map

\[
g:(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})_{d-p}
\longrightarrow
(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})_d
\]

therefore satisfies

\[
\lambda_{g,d}(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})
\le
\min\left\{
\binom n{d-p}^2,
\binom nd^2
\right\}
=
\left(\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}\right)^2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

If a decomposition has `r` Chow terms, subquotient monotonicity and the exact
one-term envelope give

\[
\lambda_{g,d}(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})
\le
r\,\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}.
\]

Hence the lower bound obtained from this profile is at most

\[
\left\lceil
\frac{\lambda_{g,d}(A_{\operatorname{perm}_n})}
{\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}}
\right\rceil
\le
\beta^{\mathrm{pr}}_{n,p,d}
\le
\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

This proves the claimed general-`n` barrier.

## 5. What has actually been closed

The theorem covers all profiles of the form

\[
\dim\bigl((g)A_f\bigr)_d
\]

where `g` is an arbitrary nonzero binary form in the selected differential
two-plane. In particular it covers:

- every power `L^p` of an arbitrary direction;
- every reducible or irreducible binary form `g(s,t)`;
- every principal homogeneous ideal after an arbitrary change of basis in
  `W`; and
- every truncated ideal profile for which only one minimal generator is active
  at the tested degree.

It does **not** cover an ideal such as

\[
(g_1,g_2)
\]

when the two generator images are both genuinely needed. The sum of two images
contains relative-intersection information absent from either principal map.
That is now the first open two-direction ideal interface.

## 6. Exact finite replay

The primary replay builds the characteristic-zero inclusion matrix for

\[
L^p:(B_n)_{d-p}\to(B_n)_d.
\]

After removing the harmless nonzero scalar `p!`, its entries are

\[
M_{J,I}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad I\subseteq J.
\]

For every

\[
2\le n\le10,
\qquad
1\le p\le d\le n,
\]

it verifies over the prime `1,000,003` that the rank is exactly the smaller
binomial coefficient. Since modular rank is a lower bound for characteristic-
zero rank and the source/target dimensions give the matching upper bound, this
is an exact replay.

```text
principal profile cells checked = 219
```

A second implementation uses a different prime and independently reconstructs
the subset-containment matrices for `2<=n<=8`.

The finite computation is a regression for the explicit interface. The
general theorem rests on the characteristic-zero `sl_2` argument, not on
finite extrapolation.
