# Shifted partial derivatives stop below lower 26 for `perm_6`

## Status

`PURE_DIMENSION_THEOREM`, `EXACT_INTEGER_REPLAY`, `ROUTE_CEILING`
(N6-034).

This note treats every shifted-partial-derivative flattening, not a finite
selection of shifts.  It proves a limitation of the method and does not
determine the Chow rank.  The current ordinary interval remains

\[
26\leq\operatorname{ChowRank}(\operatorname{perm}_6)\leq32.
\]

## 1. The maps

Let `V` be the 36-dimensional variable space.  For a sextic `f`, an output
derivative degree `0<=m<=6`, and a shift `ell>=0`, define

\[
\Phi_{m,\ell}(f):
 S^{6-m}V^*\otimes S^\ell V
 \longrightarrow S^{m+\ell}V
\tag{1.1}
\]

by taking the `(6-m)`-th derivative and multiplying it by the shift factor.
Equivalently,

\[
\operatorname{im}\Phi_{m,\ell}(f)
=S^\ell V\cdot\mathcal D_m(f).
\tag{1.2}
\]

This is the shifted-partial Young flattening in Guan's terminology.  The map
depends linearly on `f`, so matrix-rank subadditivity gives a Chow-rank lower
bound after division by the maximum rank of one Chow term.

### Theorem 1.1

For every `0<=m<=6` and every integer `ell>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{m,\ell}(\operatorname{perm}_6)}
{\max_T\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{m,\ell}(T)}
\leq
\frac{843600}{35009}<25,
}
\tag{1.3}
\]

where `T` ranges over all nonzero degree-six Chow terms.  Equality in the
displayed upper bound on the right occurs for the source-target dimension
estimate at `(m,ell)=(3,3)`; no equality is claimed for the actual permanent
rank.

Consequently the entire shifted-partial family can certify at most Chow rank
25 for `perm_6`.  In particular, it cannot reprove the current lower bound 26
and cannot prove lower 27.

## 2. Exact one-term Hilbert function

An independent six-factor term is equivalent to

\[
T=z_1z_2\cdots z_6.
\]

Its degree-`m` derivative space is spanned by the squarefree degree-`m`
monomials in the six active variables.  Thus (1.2) is the degree
`D=m+ell` part of the monomial ideal generated by those squarefree
monomials.

A degree-`D` monomial lies in that ideal precisely when at least `m` of the
six active variables occur.  If exactly `s` occur, first choose their support,
put one copy of each into the monomial, and distribute the remaining `D-s`
copies among those `s` variables and the 30 inactive variables.  Therefore

\[
B_{m,\ell}:=\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{m,\ell}(T)
=\sum_{s=m}^{\min(6,D)}
 \binom6s\binom{D+29}{s+29}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

For `m=0`, this is simply `dim S^ell V`.

Independent ordered factor tuples form a dense open subset of `V^6`.
Dependent tuples are specializations, and the rank of the fixed linear map
(1.1) cannot increase under specialization.  Hence (2.1) is the maximum
over all Chow terms, including terms with dependent or repeated factors.

## 3. Permanent source-target bound

The derivative-tower theorem gives

\[
\dim\mathcal D_m(\operatorname{perm}_6)=\binom6m^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\operatorname{rank}\Phi_{m,\ell}(\operatorname{perm}_6)
\leq U_{m,\ell},
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
U_{m,\ell}
=\min\left\{
 \binom6m^2\binom{35+\ell}{\ell},
 \binom{35+m+\ell}{m+\ell}
\right\}.
\tag{3.2}

Both entries are dimensions: the first is the source after factoring through
the derivative image, and the second is the target.

## 4. Finite core and infinite tail

Equations (2.1)--(3.2) are integers.  Direct evaluation for

\[
0\leq m\leq6,
\qquad 0\leq D=m+\ell\leq52
\tag{4.1}
\]

gives

\[
\max\frac{U_{m,\ell}}{B_{m,\ell}}
=\frac{843600}{35009}
\quad\text{at }(m,\ell)=(3,3).
\tag{4.2}

This is a finite check of 350 states using exact integers and reduced
fractions.

It remains to control all larger shifts.  When `D>=6`, retain only the
`s=6` summand in (2.1) and use the target side of (3.2):

\[
\frac{U_{m,\ell}}{B_{m,\ell}}
\leq
R(D):=
\frac{\binom{D+35}{35}}{\binom{D+29}{35}}.
\tag{4.3}

The ratio is strictly decreasing because

\[
\frac{R(D+1)}{R(D)}
=\frac{(D+36)(D-5)}{(D+1)(D+30)}<1.
\tag{4.4}

At the first unenumerated degree,

\[
R(53)=\frac{1138511}{48230}
<\frac{843600}{35009}.
\tag{4.5}

Equations (4.2)--(4.5) prove Theorem 1.1 for every shift.

## 5. Reproduction and boundary

Run

```text
python scripts/n6_shifted_partial_ceiling.py \
  --json data/n6_shifted_partial_ceiling.json
python -m unittest tests.test_n6_shifted_partial_ceiling -v
```

Expected marker:

```text
N6_ALL_SHIFTED_PARTIAL_CEILING_PASS
```

The proof uses no random sample, floating-point rank, finite-field inference,
GPU computation, or external certificate.  The finite core is exact integer
arithmetic; the infinite tail is the symbolic inequality (4.4).

What is proved:

- every shifted-partial flattening has integer lower-bound ceiling 25 for
  `perm_6`;
- the maximum one-term rank formula is exact for arbitrary Chow terms.

What is not proved:

- that the actual maximum permanent ratio equals (4.2);
- any new ordinary or border Chow-rank bound;
- a ceiling for other Schur functors, coupled derivative degrees, recursive
  maps, quotient refinements, or nonlinear secant equations.
